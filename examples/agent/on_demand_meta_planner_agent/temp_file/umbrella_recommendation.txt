# Umbrella Recommendation for The Met Visit on June 23, 2026

## Weather Context for Late June in New York City

Since specific weather forecasts are unavailable for June 23, 2026 (as forecasts typically extend only 7-14 days ahead), this recommendation is based on historical climate patterns for New York City in late June:

- **Average precipitation**: 3.5-4.5 inches for the entire month of June
- **Rainy days**: Approximately 10-12 days with measurable precipitation during June
- **Probability of rain on any given day**: Roughly 33-40% chance
- **Typical afternoon conditions**: Warm (80-83°F/27-28°C) and sometimes humid

## Recommendation

**Yes, bring a compact umbrella or have a rain contingency plan.**

While not guaranteed to rain, there is a significant chance (approximately 1 in 3) of precipitation during your visit. Late June in NYC can experience sudden summer thunderstorms, particularly in the afternoon when heat and humidity build up.

## Practical Planning Advice

1. **Pack light but prepared**: Bring a small, collapsible umbrella that fits easily in a bag or purse.

2. **Check closer to date**: Monitor the forecast starting about 7-10 days before your trip, with increasing accuracy as you get within 3-5 days of your visit.

3. **Have backup options**: The Met is an indoor attraction, so even if it rains, you can still enjoy your visit. However, you'll need protection getting to/from the museum and possibly between transportation points.

4. **Consider waterproof alternatives**: A lightweight rain jacket might be more versatile than an umbrella, especially if you plan to do other outdoor activities.

5. **Transportation planning**: If rain is in the forecast closer to your travel date, consider direct transportation options that minimize walking outdoors (e.g., rideshare directly to the museum entrance rather than subway + walking).

6. **Museum amenities**: The Met has coat check facilities where you can store wet umbrellas or rain gear during your visit.

Given the moderate probability of rain and the inconvenience of being caught unprepared in a summer downpour, the minimal burden of carrying a compact umbrella is outweighed by the benefit of staying dry.
