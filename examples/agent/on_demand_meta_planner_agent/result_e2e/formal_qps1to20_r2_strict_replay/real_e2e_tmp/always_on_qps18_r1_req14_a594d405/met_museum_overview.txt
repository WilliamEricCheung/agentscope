# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: Concise Overview

## History
The Metropolitan Museum of Art (the Met) was founded in 1870 by a group of Americans including philanthropists, artists, and businessmen with the goal of creating a national institution to inspire and educate the public. The museum first opened on February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue, before moving to its current location in 1880. The founders included notable figures such as Theodore Roosevelt Sr. (father of President Theodore Roosevelt), railroad executive John Taylor Johnston, and artist Eastman Johnson.

## Significance
- The Met is the largest art museum in the Americas and the fourth-largest museum in the world by floor area
- It houses approximately 1.5 million works of art across 17 curatorial departments
- The museum attracts over 5.7 million visitors annually, making it the most-visited museum in the United States
- It serves as an encyclopedic art museum with collections spanning from ancient civilizations to contemporary art

## Main Collections
The Met's collection is organized into 17 curatorial departments:

### Geographically Designated Collections:
- **Ancient Near Eastern Art**: Over 7,000 pieces from Sumerian, Hittite, Assyrian, Babylonian, and other cultures
- **Arts of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas**: More than 11,000 pieces housed in the Michael C. Rockefeller Wing
- **Asian Art**: Over 35,000 pieces spanning 4,000 years, including comprehensive collections of Chinese, Indian, and Cambodian art
- **Egyptian Art**: More than 26,000 objects including the famous Temple of Dendur and ancient wooden models
- **European Paintings**: Over 2,600 works from the 13th through early 20th centuries, including masterpieces by Old Masters and Impressionists
- **Greek and Roman Art**: More than 17,000 objects including the Monteleone chariot and Cycladic sculptures
- **Islamic Art**: One of the world's largest collections with 12,000 objects from Spain to Central Asia

### Non-Geographically Designated Collections:
- **American Wing**: American art from the 18th through early 20th century
- **Arms and Armor**: 14,000 objects including armor belonging to European royalty
- **Costume Institute**: Over 35,000 costumes and accessories, famous for the annual Met Gala
- **Modern and Contemporary Art**: 13,000 artworks including iconic pieces by Picasso, Pollock, and Johns
- **Musical Instruments**: 5,000 instruments from around the world, including Stradivari violins
- **Photographs**: Over 25,000 photographs including works by Stieglitz and Steichen
- **Robert Lehman Collection**: 2,600 works considered "one of the most extraordinary private art collections ever assembled"

## Location
- **Main Building**: 1000 Fifth Avenue, along Museum Mile on the eastern edge of Central Park in Manhattan's Upper East Side
- **The Cloisters**: Located in Fort Tryon Park in Upper Manhattan, dedicated to medieval European art
- **Future Location**: The Neue Galerie New York will become the Met's third location in 2028 following a planned merger

The main building spans approximately 2 million square feet and was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1986.
