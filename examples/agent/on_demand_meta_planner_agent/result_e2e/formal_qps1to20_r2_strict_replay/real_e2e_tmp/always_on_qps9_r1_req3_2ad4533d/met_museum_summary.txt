# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: Key Information Summary

## History and Founding
- Founded in 1870 by a group of Americans including philanthropists, artists, and businessmen
- Officially incorporated on April 13, 1870, with the goal of creating a national institution to inspire and educate the public
- First opened on February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue
- Moved to its current location at 1000 Fifth Avenue in 1880
- Key founders included John Taylor Johnston (first president), George Palmer Putnam (founding superintendent), Eastman Johnson, Frederic Edwin Church, and Henry Gurdon Marquand
- Luigi Palma di Cesnola served as the first director from 1879 to 1904

## Significance and Scale
- Colloquially known as "the Met"
- Fourth-largest museum in the world by floor area
- Largest art museum in the Americas
- Most-visited museum in the United States (5,727,258 visitors in fiscal year 2025)
- Fifth-most visited art museum in the world
- Main building contains approximately 2 million square feet (190,000 m²) of floor space
- Collection currently lists 1.5 million works (down from over 2 million in 2000)
- Organized into 17 curatorial departments

## Locations
1. **Main Building**: 1000 Fifth Avenue, along Museum Mile on Manhattan's Upper East Side
2. **The Cloisters**: Located in Fort Tryon Park in Upper Manhattan, dedicated to medieval European art, architecture, and artifacts
3. **Future Location**: Set to merge with the Neue Galerie New York in 2028, which will become the Met's newest location focusing on early 20th century Austrian and German art

## Main Collections

### Geographically Designated Collections

**Ancient Near Eastern Art**
- Over 7,000 pieces from the Neolithic Period through the end of Late Antiquity
- Includes works from Sumerian, Hittite, Sasanian, Assyrian, Babylonian, and Elamite cultures
- Highlights: Sumerian Stele of Ushumgal, Elamite silver Kneeling Bull with Vessel, Pratt Ivories, and monumental stone lamassu from Assyrian king Ashurnasirpal II

**Arts of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas**
- Over 11,000 pieces housed in the 40,000-square-foot Michael C. Rockefeller Wing
- Major collection began in 1969 with Nelson A. Rockefeller's donation of over 3,000 pieces
- Ranges from 40,000-year-old indigenous Australian rock paintings to 15-foot-tall memorial poles from New Guinea
- Includes ceremonial objects from the Nigerian Court of Benin

**Asian Art**
- Over 35,000 pieces, considered the most comprehensive Asian art collection in the US
- Spans 4,000 years of Asian art
- Strong holdings in Cambodian, Indian, Chinese, Nepalese, Tibetan, Burmese, and Thai art
- Features the Astor Court, a complete Ming Dynasty-style garden court

**Egyptian Art**
- Over 26,000 pieces from the Paleolithic era through the Ptolemaic era
- Almost half acquired through the museum's own archaeological excavations (1906-1941)
- Centerpiece: Temple of Dendur, given to the US in 1965 and assembled at the Met in 1978
- Includes William the Faience Hippopotamus (informal mascot of the museum)

**European Paintings**
- Over 2,600 works from the 13th through early 20th century
- Strong collections of Old Masters, Impressionist, and Post-Impressionist works
- Notable strengths in works by Courbet, Degas, Manet, Monet, Cézanne, and Van Gogh
- Major collections include the Havemeyer Collection and the Annenberg Collection

**Greek and Roman Art**
- Over 17,000 objects dating back to the founding of the museum
- First accessioned object was a Roman sarcophagus (still on display)
- Highlights include the Amathus sarcophagus, Monteleone chariot, and early Cycladic sculptures
- Features a reconstructed bedroom from a villa in Boscoreale, buried by Vesuvius in 79 CE

**Islamic Art**
- One of the world's largest collections of Islamic art (12,000+ objects)
- Includes both religious and secular items from Spain to Central Asia
- Strong collection of miniature paintings from Iran and Mughal India
- Features the reconstructed Nur Al-Din Room from an 18th-century house in Damascus

### Non-Geographically Designated Collections

**American Wing**
- Comprehensive collection of American art from the 18th through early 20th century
- Added Indigenous American art in 2018 with the "Art of Native America" exhibition

**Arms and Armor**
- One of the museum's most popular collections with 14,000 objects
- Strongest in late medieval European and Japanese pieces (5th-19th centuries)
- Includes armor belonging to Henry VIII of England, Henry II of France, and Ferdinand I

**Costume Institute**
- Over 35,000 costumes and accessories
- Known for the annual Met Gala and special exhibitions like "Alexander McQueen: Savage Beauty"

**Modern and Contemporary Art**
- 13,000 artworks primarily by European and American artists
- Iconic works include Picasso's portrait of Gertrude Stein, Jasper Johns's White Flag, and Jackson Pollock's Autumn Rhythm
- Significant Cubist collection donated by Leonard Lauder in 2013 (valued at $1 billion)

**Robert Lehman Collection**
- 2,600 works donated in 1969, described as "one of the most extraordinary private art collections ever assembled in the United States"
- Particularly strong in Italian Renaissance paintings, especially the Sienese school

**Medieval Art and The Cloisters**
- Over 10,000 objects total (6,000 in main building, 5,000 at The Cloisters)
- The Cloisters features the Belles Heures manuscript, the "Cloisters Cross," and the Hunt of the Unicorn tapestries

**Other Notable Collections**
- Musical Instruments: 5,000 examples from around the world, including Stradivari violins and the oldest surviving piano (1720)
- Photographs: Over 25,000 works including major collections from Alfred Stieglitz and Walker Evans
- Drawings and Prints: 21,000 drawings, 1.2 million prints, and 12,000 illustrated books

## Architecture
- Original building designed by Calvert Vaux and Jacob Wrey Mould (1880)
- Fifth Avenue facade, Great Hall, and Grand Stairway designed by Richard Morris Hunt in Beaux-Arts style
- Wings completed by McKim, Mead & White in 1910
- Modern additions by Roche-Dinkeloo
- Designated a National Historic Landmark in 1986

## Recent Developments and Challenges
- Changed admission policy in 2018 to charge $25 for out-of-state visitors
- Temporarily closed during COVID-19 pandemic (March 2020)
- Facing ongoing challenges related to repatriation of looted artifacts
- Has returned numerous items to countries including Italy, Egypt, Cambodia, Thailand, and Greece
- Implemented new collecting practices emphasizing research, transparency, and collaboration
