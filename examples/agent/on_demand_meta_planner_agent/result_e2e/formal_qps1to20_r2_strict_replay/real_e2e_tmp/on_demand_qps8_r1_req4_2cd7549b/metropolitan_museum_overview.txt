# The Metropolitan Museum of Art - Overview

## History
- Founded in 1870 by a group of American citizens including businessmen, financiers, and leading artists
- Opened to the public on February 20, 1872, initially at 681 Fifth Avenue
- Moved to its current location on Fifth Avenue along the eastern edge of Central Park in 1880
- The main building was designed by architects Calvert Vaux and Jacob Wrey Mould
- Has undergone numerous expansions over the decades, including the addition of The Met Cloisters in 1938 (dedicated to medieval art)

## Significance
- One of the largest art museums in the world by both area and collection size
- Houses over 2 million works spanning 5,000 years of world culture
- Serves as a major cultural institution and tourist destination, attracting millions of visitors annually
- Plays a crucial role in art education, conservation, and scholarship
- Represents a comprehensive survey of human artistic achievement

## Main Collections
- **European Paintings**: Masterpieces by Rembrandt, Vermeer, Van Gogh, Monet, and many others
- **American Wing**: American decorative arts, paintings, and sculpture from the colonial period through the early 20th century
- **Ancient Egyptian Art**: One of the finest collections outside Cairo, including the Temple of Dendur
- **Greek and Roman Art**: Sculpture, vases, and artifacts from antiquity
- **Asian Art**: Comprehensive collections from China, Japan, Korea, India, and Southeast Asia
- **Islamic Art**: Works spanning 1,400 years from Spain to India
- **Arms and Armor**: Medieval and Renaissance European arms and armor
- **Costume Institute**: Fashion and costume design (hosts the famous Met Gala)
- **Modern and Contemporary Art**: Works from the 20th and 21st centuries
- **Photographs**: Historic and contemporary photography
- **Musical Instruments**: Rare and historic instruments from around the world
