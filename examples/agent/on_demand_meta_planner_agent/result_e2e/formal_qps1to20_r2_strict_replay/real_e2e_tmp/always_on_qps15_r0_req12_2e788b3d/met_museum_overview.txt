# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: Overview

## History
The Metropolitan Museum of Art (the Met) was founded in 1870 by a group of American philanthropists, artists, and businessmen with the goal of creating a national institution to inspire and educate the public. The museum first opened on February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue, before moving to its current location on Fifth Avenue along Central Park's eastern edge in 1880. The founders included notable figures such as Theodore Roosevelt Sr. (father of President Theodore Roosevelt), railroad executive John Taylor Johnston, and artist Eastman Johnson.

## Significance
- **Scale**: The Met is the largest art museum in the Americas and the fourth-largest museum in the world by floor area, with approximately 2 million square feet of space.
- **Visitation**: It is the most-visited museum in the United States, attracting over 5.7 million visitors annually (as of fiscal year 2025).
- **Collection Size**: The museum houses approximately 1.5 million works of art spanning 5,000 years of world culture.
- **Organization**: The collection is organized into 17 curatorial departments, making it one of the most comprehensive encyclopedic art museums globally.

## Main Collections

### Ancient Art
- **Egyptian Art**: Over 26,000 pieces from the Paleolithic era through the Ptolemaic era, including the iconic Temple of Dendur and the beloved "William the Faience Hippopotamus."
- **Greek and Roman Art**: More than 17,000 objects including the Monteleone chariot and Cycladic sculptures.
- **Ancient Near Eastern Art**: Over 7,000 pieces representing Sumerian, Hittite, Assyrian, Babylonian, and other cultures.

### European Art
- **European Paintings**: Over 2,600 works from the 13th through early 20th centuries, including masterpieces by Rembrandt, Vermeer, and the Impressionists.
- **Impressionist and Post-Impressionist**: One of the world's great repositories featuring works by Monet, Degas, Van Gogh, Cézanne, and Gauguin.
- **European Sculpture and Decorative Arts**: Over 50,000 pieces from the 15th through early 20th centuries, including period rooms and Renaissance sculpture.

### American Art
- **American Wing**: Comprehensive collection of American art from the 18th through early 20th centuries.
- **Native American Art**: Added in 2018 with the "Art of Native America" exhibition.

### Global Collections
- **Asian Art**: Over 35,000 pieces spanning 4,000 years, including Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Indian, and Southeast Asian works.
- **Arts of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas**: Over 11,000 pieces housed in the Michael C. Rockefeller Wing.
- **Islamic Art**: 12,000 works from Spain to Central Asia, including miniature paintings and calligraphy.

### Specialized Collections
- **Arms and Armor**: 14,000 objects from around the world, including armor belonging to European royalty.
- **Costume Institute**: Over 35,000 costumes and accessories, famous for the annual Met Gala.
- **Musical Instruments**: 5,000 instruments from around the world, including Stradivari violins.
- **Drawings and Prints**: 21,000 drawings and 1.2 million prints.

### Modern and Contemporary Art
- Approximately 13,000 works by European and American artists, including Picasso's portrait of Gertrude Stein and Jackson Pollock's "Autumn Rhythm."

## Additional Locations
- **The Cloisters**: Located in Fort Tryon Park, dedicated to medieval European art and architecture.
- **Met Breuer** (2016-2020): Formerly housed modern and contemporary art in the former Whitney Museum building.
- **Future Expansion**: Set to merge with the Neue Galerie New York in 2028, adding Austrian and German early 20th-century art.

The Met continues to serve as a vital cultural institution, balancing its role as a preserver of global artistic heritage with ongoing efforts to address issues of provenance, representation, and accessibility in the modern museum landscape.
