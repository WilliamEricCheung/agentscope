# Research on "The Great Wave off Kanagawa" Woodblock Print

## Basic Information
- **Full Title**: Under the Wave off Kanagawa (Kanagawa oki nami ura), or The Great Wave, from the series Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji (Fugaku sanjūrokkei)
- **Artist**: Katsushika Hokusai (Japanese, Tokyo/Edo 1760–1849 Tokyo/Edo)
- **Series**: Part of "Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji" (Fugaku sanjūrokkei)
- **Medium**: Woodblock print; ink and color on paper

## Physical Details
- **Dimensions**: Approximately 10 x 15 inches (25.4 x 38.1 cm)
- **Department**: Asian Art (Metropolitan Museum of Art)

## Historical Context
"The Great Wave off Kanagawa" is arguably the most famous work by Japanese artist Katsushika Hokusai and one of the most recognizable works of Japanese art in the world. Created during the Edo period (around 1830-1833), this woodblock print represents the pinnacle of ukiyo-e art.

The print depicts an enormous wave threatening boats off the coast of Kanagawa (near present-day Yokohama), with Mount Fuji visible in the background. The dramatic composition showcases Hokusai's mastery of perspective and his innovative use of Prussian blue pigment, which was relatively new to Japan at the time.

## Cultural Significance
- This work exemplifies the Japanese concept of nature's power and human vulnerability
- Mount Fuji, Japan's sacred mountain, appears small and distant compared to the overwhelming wave, suggesting both the mountain's eternal nature and humanity's temporary existence
- The print influenced many Western artists, particularly the Impressionists and Post-Impressionists
- It represents the fusion of traditional Japanese artistic techniques with new materials and perspectives

## Metropolitan Museum Collection
The Met Museum holds multiple versions of this iconic print, demonstrating how woodblock prints could vary slightly between different printings due to:
- Different paper quality
- Variations in ink application
- Wear on the wooden blocks over time
- Different color schemes used in various editions

Two notable versions in the Met collection include:
1. The Howard Mansfield Collection version (Object ID: 39799) - acquired through Purchase, Rogers Fund, 1936
2. The H.O. Havemeyer Collection version (Object ID: 45434) - bequeathed in 1929

Both versions show slight dimensional differences, which is typical for woodblock prints from this era.

## Technical Aspects
- Created using the traditional Japanese woodblock printing technique (mokuhanga)
- Used multiple carved wooden blocks - one for each color plus the key block for outlines
- Featured Prussian blue (bero-ai), a synthetic pigment imported from Europe that revolutionized Japanese printmaking
- The composition demonstrates sophisticated understanding of perspective, possibly influenced by Western art

## Legacy
"The Great Wave" has become a global cultural icon, appearing in countless reproductions, adaptations, and references in popular culture. Its distinctive wave form has been imitated and referenced by artists worldwide, making it one of the most influential images in art history.
