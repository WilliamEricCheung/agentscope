# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: A Concise Overview

## History
The Metropolitan Museum of Art (the Met) was founded in 1870 when the New York State Legislature granted it an Act of Incorporation "for the purpose of establishing and maintaining in said City a Museum and Library of Art." The museum was established by a group of Americans including philanthropists, artists, and businessmen who wanted to create a national institution that would inspire and educate the public.

Key historical milestones:
- Opened on February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue
- Moved to its current location at 1000 Fifth Avenue in 1880
- Founded by notable figures including John Taylor Johnston (first president), George Palmer Putnam (founding superintendent), and artists Eastman Johnson and Frederic Edwin Church
- Theodore Roosevelt Sr. (father of President Theodore Roosevelt) was among the founders
- Luigi Palma di Cesnola served as its first director from 1879 to 1904

## Significance
- Fourth-largest museum in the world by floor area
- Largest art museum in the Americas
- Most-visited museum in the United States (5.7 million visitors in fiscal year 2025)
- Fifth-most visited art museum in the world
- Houses approximately 1.5 million works of art across 17 curatorial departments
- Main building spans approximately 2 million square feet
- Designated a National Historic Landmark in 1986

## Main Collections

### Geographically Designated Collections
1. **Ancient Near Eastern Art**: Over 7,000 pieces spanning from the Neolithic Period through the fall of the Sasanian Empire, including works from Sumerian, Hittite, Assyrian, Babylonian, and Elamite cultures.

2. **Arts of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas**: Over 11,000 pieces housed in the Michael C. Rockefeller Wing, featuring works from sub-Saharan Africa, the Pacific Islands, and the Americas dating from 3,000 BCE to present.

3. **Asian Art**: One of the most comprehensive collections in the US with over 35,000 pieces spanning 4,000 years, including significant holdings of Chinese, Indian, Cambodian, Japanese, Korean, and Southeast Asian art.

4. **Egyptian Art**: Over 26,000 pieces from the Paleolithic era through the Ptolemaic era, displayed in 40 Egyptian galleries. The centerpiece is the Temple of Dendur, given to the US by Egypt in 1965.

5. **European Paintings**: Over 2,600 works from the 13th through early 20th century, including masterpieces by Rembrandt, Vermeer, Velázquez, and extensive Impressionist and Post-Impressionist works by Monet, Degas, Van Gogh, and others.

6. **Greek and Roman Art**: Over 17,000 objects including the monumental Amathus sarcophagus and the Monteleone chariot.

7. **Islamic Art**: 12,000 works spanning from Spain to Central Asia, including miniature paintings, ceramics, textiles, and the reconstructed Nur Al-Din Room from Damascus.

### Non-Geographically Designated Collections
1. **American Wing**: Comprehensive collection of American art from the 18th through early 20th century, including the 2018 addition of Indigenous American art.

2. **Arms and Armor**: 14,000 objects spanning cultures and time periods, including armor belonging to European royalty.

3. **Costume Institute**: Over 35,000 costumes and accessories, featured in special exhibitions and the annual Met Gala.

4. **Drawings and Prints**: About 21,000 drawings and 1.2 million prints from Europe and the Americas.

5. **Robert Lehman Collection**: 2,600 works donated in 1969, considered "one of the most extraordinary private art collections ever assembled in the United States."

6. **Medieval Art**: Over 10,000 objects divided between the main building and The Cloisters, which houses medieval European art in a building incorporating five French cloisters.

7. **Modern and Contemporary Art**: 13,000 works including iconic pieces by Picasso, Pollock, and Johns, plus a significant Cubist collection donated by Leonard Lauder.

8. **Musical Instruments**: 5,000 instruments from around the world, including Stradivari violins and the oldest surviving piano (1720).

9. **Photographs**: Over 25,000 photographs including major collections from Alfred Stieglitz and Walker Evans.

## Additional Locations
- **The Cloisters**: Located in Fort Tryon Park, dedicated exclusively to medieval art
- **Met Breuer** (2016-2020): Formerly housed modern and contemporary art in the former Whitney Museum building
- **Neue Galerie** (merging in 2028): Will become the Met's third location, focusing on early 20th century Austrian and German art
