# Research on "The Great Wave off Kanagawa" Woodblock Print

## Overview
"The Great Wave off Kanagawa" (Japanese: 神奈川沖浪裏, Kanagawa oki nami ura) is one of the most iconic works of Japanese art and possibly the most famous woodblock print in the world. It was created by Katsushika Hokusai as part of his series "Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji" (Fugaku sanjūrokkei).

## Artist Information
- **Name**: Katsushika Hokusai
- **Life**: 1760–1849, Tokyo (Edo), Japan
- **Period**: Edo period

## Historical Context and Significance
"The Great Wave" was created around 1830-1833 during the Edo period in Japan. It represents the dramatic power of nature with its towering wave threatening three boats, while Mount Fuji appears small and distant in the background, symbolizing the eternal and unchanging nature of the sacred mountain compared to the transient and dangerous sea.

The print is notable for its use of Prussian blue pigment (bero-ai), which was a relatively new import to Japan at the time and became extremely popular in ukiyo-e prints.

## Preservation Notes
The Met holds multiple impressions of this famous print, which is common for popular ukiyo-e works. Different impressions may show variations in color intensity and condition due to:
1. The number of prints pulled from the original woodblocks
2. The quality of paper used
3. Environmental conditions during storage over nearly 200 years
4. Previous restoration efforts

Both versions in the Met's collection are preserved in the Department of Asian Art, with careful attention to light exposure, humidity, and temperature to prevent further deterioration of the paper and pigments.

## Cultural Impact
"The Great Wave" has had an enormous influence on Western art, particularly on the Impressionists and Post-Impressionists like Monet, Van Gogh, and Debussy (who composed "La Mer" partly inspired by this image). It remains one of the most reproduced and referenced artworks in global popular culture.
