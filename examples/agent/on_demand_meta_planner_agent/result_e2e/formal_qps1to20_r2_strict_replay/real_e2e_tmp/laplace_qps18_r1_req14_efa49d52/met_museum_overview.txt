# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: Overview

## History and Founding
The Metropolitan Museum of Art (the Met) was founded in 1870 by a group of Americans including philanthropists, artists, and businessmen with the goal of creating a national institution that would inspire and educate the public. The museum first opened on February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue, before moving to its current location in 1880. Key founders included John Taylor Johnston (a railroad executive whose personal art collection seeded the museum), George Palmer Putnam (publisher), Eastman Johnson (artist), and Frederic Edwin Church (landscape painter). Theodore Roosevelt Sr., father of President Theodore Roosevelt, was also among the founders.

## Significance
The Met is one of the world's most important cultural institutions:
- By floor area, it is the fourth-largest museum in the world and the largest art museum in the Americas
- It was the most-visited museum in the United States and the fifth-most visited art museum in the world (5,727,258 visitors in fiscal year 2025)
- The museum building was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1986
- It houses approximately 1.5 million works of art spanning 5,000 years of world culture

## Main Collections
The Met's encyclopedic collection is divided into 17 curatorial departments:

1. **Ancient Near Eastern Art**: Over 7,000 pieces from Sumerian, Hittite, Assyrian, Babylonian, and other cultures
2. **Arts of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas**: More than 11,000 pieces housed in the Michael C. Rockefeller Wing
3. **Asian Art**: Over 35,000 pieces spanning 4,000 years, including comprehensive collections of Chinese, Indian, and Cambodian art
4. **Egyptian Art**: More than 26,000 objects, including the iconic Temple of Dendur
5. **European Paintings**: Over 2,600 works from the 13th through early 20th centuries, including masterpieces by Rembrandt, Vermeer, and many Impressionist and Post-Impressionist artists
6. **American Wing**: Comprehensive collection of American art from the 18th through early 20th century
7. **Greek and Roman Art**: More than 17,000 objects dating from the Neolithic period through the Roman Empire
8. **Islamic Art**: One of the world's largest collections of Islamic art with 12,000 objects
9. **Arms and Armor**: 14,000 objects spanning cultures from ancient Egypt to 19th-century America
10. **Costume Institute**: Over 35,000 costumes and accessories
11. **Drawings and Prints**: About 21,000 drawings and 1.2 million prints
12. **Robert Lehman Collection**: 2,600 works donated by banker Robert Lehman
13. **Medieval Art**: Over 10,000 objects split between the main building and The Cloisters
14. **Modern and Contemporary Art**: 13,000 works including iconic pieces by Picasso, Pollock, and Johns
15. **Musical Instruments**: 5,000 instruments from around the world
16. **Photographs**: Over 25,000 photographs
17. **European Sculpture and Decorative Arts**: Over 50,000 pieces from the 15th through early 20th centuries

## Facilities
- **Main Building**: Located at 1000 Fifth Avenue along Museum Mile on Manhattan's Upper East Side, with approximately 2 million square feet of space
- **The Cloisters**: Located in Fort Tryon Park, dedicated exclusively to medieval European art and architecture
- **Future Third Location**: The Met will merge with the Neue Galerie New York in 2028, adding its collection of early 20th-century Austrian and German art
