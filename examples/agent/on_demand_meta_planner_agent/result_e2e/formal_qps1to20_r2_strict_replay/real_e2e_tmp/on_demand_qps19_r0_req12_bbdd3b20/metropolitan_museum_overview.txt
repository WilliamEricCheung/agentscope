# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: Overview

## Location
The Metropolitan Museum of Art (The Met) is located on the eastern edge of Central Park along Manhattan's Museum Mile in New York City, at 1000 Fifth Avenue.

## History
- Founded in 1870 by a group of American citizens including businessmen, financiers, and leading artists
- Opened to the public on February 20, 1872
- Has grown from its original location at 681 Fifth Avenue to its current iconic building designed by Richard Morris Hunt

## Significance
- Largest art museum in the United States
- One of the most visited museums in the world
- Houses over 2 million works of art spanning 5,000 years of world culture
- Serves as a cultural landmark and educational institution

## Main Collections
- **Ancient Art**: Egyptian artifacts, Greek and Roman antiquities
- **European Art**: Works from the Renaissance through the 19th century, including masterpieces by Rembrandt, Vermeer, and others
- **American Art**: Paintings, sculptures, and decorative arts from the colonial period to the early 20th century
- **Modern and Contemporary Art**: Works from the 20th and 21st centuries
- **Asian Art**: Comprehensive collection spanning China, Japan, Korea, India, and Southeast Asia
- **Islamic Art**: Artworks from the 7th to the 19th century
- **African, Oceanic, and Americas Art**: Traditional and contemporary works
- **Costume Institute**: Fashion and costume design
- **Musical Instruments**: Historical and cultural instruments from around the world
- **Arms and Armor**: European, Japanese, and Islamic arms and armor

The Met continues to be a vital cultural institution, offering exhibitions, educational programs, and preserving artistic heritage for future generations.
