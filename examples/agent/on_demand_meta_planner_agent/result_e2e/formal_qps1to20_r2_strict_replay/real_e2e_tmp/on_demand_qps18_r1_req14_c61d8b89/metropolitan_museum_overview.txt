# The Metropolitan Museum of Art - Comprehensive Overview

## History
The Metropolitan Museum of Art was founded in 1870 by a group of American citizens including businessmen, financiers, and leading artists who wanted to bring art and art education to the American people. The museum opened to the public on February 20, 1872, initially located at 681 Fifth Avenue in a former dancing academy. In 1880, the museum moved to its current location on Fifth Avenue along the eastern edge of Central Park, where it has continued to expand and grow into one of the world's premier cultural institutions.

## Significance
The Met is one of the world's largest and most comprehensive art museums, housing over 2 million works of art spanning 5,000 years of world culture from every part of the globe. Its collection includes:

- Ancient Egyptian artifacts and temples
- European paintings from the Old Masters to Impressionists
- American art from colonial times to the early 20th century
- Extensive Asian art collections
- Islamic art and artifacts
- Greek and Roman antiquities
- Modern and contemporary art
- Costume Institute with fashion history
- Musical instruments and decorative arts

The museum also operates The Met Cloisters in Fort Tryon Park, which is dedicated to medieval European art and architecture.

The Met serves as both a major tourist destination and an important educational and research institution, offering exhibitions, educational programs, and scholarly resources to millions of visitors annually.

## Exact Location
**Main Building:**
- Address: 1000 Fifth Avenue, New York, NY 10028
- Located on Manhattan's Upper East Side along Museum Mile
- Situated on the eastern edge of Central Park between 80th and 84th Streets

**The Met Cloisters:**
- Address: 99 Margaret Corbin Drive, Fort Tryon Park, New York, NY 10040
- Located in northern Manhattan in Fort Tryon Park overlooking the Hudson River

Both locations are easily accessible by public transportation and serve as iconic landmarks in New York City's cultural landscape.
