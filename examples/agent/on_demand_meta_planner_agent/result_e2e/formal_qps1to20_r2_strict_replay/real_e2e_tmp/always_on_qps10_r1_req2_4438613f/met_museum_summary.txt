# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: History, Significance, and Location

## History
The Metropolitan Museum of Art (the Met) was founded in 1870 when the New York State Legislature granted it an Act of Incorporation "for the purpose of establishing and maintaining in said City a Museum and Library of Art, of encouraging and developing the Study of the Fine Arts, and the application of Art to manufacture and natural life, of advancing the general knowledge of kindred subjects, and to that end of furnishing popular instruction and recreations."

The museum was established by a group of Americans including philanthropists, artists, and businessmen who wanted to create a national institution that would inspire and educate the public. Key founders included Theodore Roosevelt Sr. (father of President Theodore Roosevelt), railroad executive John Taylor Johnston (who served as the first president), publisher George Palmer Putnam, and artists Eastman Johnson and Frederic Edwin Church.

The museum first opened on February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue. In 1873, it moved to the Douglas Mansion at 128 West 14th Street to accommodate its growing collection, particularly after acquiring the Cesnola Collection of Cypriot antiquities. The museum moved to its current location on Fifth Avenue in 1880. The 1893 Act required that the museum's collections "shall be kept open and accessible to the public free of all charge throughout the year," though this policy was modified in 2018 to charge $25 for out-of-state and foreign visitors.

## Significance
The Met is one of the world's most significant cultural institutions:
- It is the fourth-largest museum in the world by floor area and the largest art museum in the Americas
- It was the most-visited museum in the United States and the fifth-most visited art museum globally, with 5,727,258 visitors in fiscal year 2025
- Its collection contains approximately 1.5 million works of art divided into 17 curatorial departments
- The collection spans 5,000 years of world culture, from prehistory to the present, representing virtually every part of the globe
- It houses encyclopedic collections including works from ancient Egypt, classical antiquity, European Old Masters, American art, modern and contemporary art, as well as extensive holdings of African, Asian, Oceanian, Byzantine, and Islamic art
- The museum also maintains comprehensive collections of musical instruments, costumes, decorative arts, textiles, and antique weapons and armor

Notable collections include the Temple of Dendur (an entire Egyptian temple gifted to the US in 1965), the Robert Lehman Collection (one of the most extraordinary private art collections ever assembled in the United States), and The Cloisters collection of medieval European art.

## Location
The Met has three locations:

1. **Main Building**: Located at 1000 Fifth Avenue along Museum Mile on the eastern edge of Central Park in Manhattan's Upper East Side. This building, which began construction in 1880, now measures almost 1/4-mile long with more than 2 million square feet of floor space—more than 20 times the size of the original 1880 building. The building is an accretion of over 20 structures designed by various architects including Calvert Vaux, Jacob Wrey Mould, Richard Morris Hunt, and the firm McKim, Mead & White.

2. **The Cloisters**: Located in Fort Tryon Park in Upper Manhattan, this branch is dedicated solely to medieval art. Completed in 1938 as a gift from John D. Rockefeller Jr., it incorporates five medieval French cloisters and houses approximately 5,000 objects of medieval European art.

3. **Neue Galerie**: Currently operating as a separate museum but set to become the Met's third location in 2028 following a planned merger. It will house the Met's collection of early 20th century Austrian and German art.
