# The Metropolitan Museum of Art

## History
- Founded in 1870 by a group of American citizens including businessmen, financiers, and leading artists
- Opened to the public on February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue
- Moved to its current location in Central Park along Manhattan's Museum Mile in 1880
- The main building was designed by architects Calvert Vaux and Jacob Wrey Mould

## Significance
- One of the world's largest art museums by area
- Houses over 2 million works spanning 5,000 years of world culture
- Receives millions of visitors annually, making it one of the most visited museums globally
- Considered one of New York City's cultural landmarks

## Main Collections
- American Decorative Arts and American Paintings and Sculpture
- Ancient Near Eastern Art
- Arms and Armor
- Arts of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas
- Asian Art (including significant Chinese, Japanese, and Korean collections)
- Costume Institute
- Drawings and Prints
- Egyptian Art (including the Temple of Dendur)
- European Paintings (featuring works by Rembrandt, Vermeer, Monet, and many others)
- European Sculpture and Decorative Arts
- Greek and Roman Art
- Islamic Art
- Medieval Art
- Modern and Contemporary Art
- Musical Instruments
- Photographs

## Additional Information
The Met also operates The Met Cloisters in Fort Tryon Park, which is dedicated to medieval European art and architecture.
