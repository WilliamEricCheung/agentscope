# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: Overview

## History
The Metropolitan Museum of Art (often referred to as "The Met") was founded in 1870 by a group of American citizens including businessmen, financiers, and leading artists. The museum opened to the public on February 20, 1872, initially housed in a building on Fifth Avenue. In 1880, it moved to its current location in Central Park along Manhattan's Museum Mile. The iconic Beaux-Arts facade was completed in 1902, designed by architect Richard Morris Hunt.

## Significance
The Met is one of the world's largest and most prestigious art museums, serving as a cultural cornerstone of New York City and the United States. It houses over 2 million works spanning 5,000 years of world culture from every part of the globe. The museum attracts millions of visitors annually and plays a crucial role in art education, conservation, and scholarship. Its mission is to collect, preserve, study, exhibit, and stimulate appreciation for works of art that collectively represent the broadest spectrum of human achievement.

## Main Collections
The Met's collections are organized into 17 curatorial departments:

1. **American Wing** - American decorative arts, paintings, and sculpture from the colonial period through the early 20th century
2. **Ancient Near Eastern Art** - Artifacts from Mesopotamia, Iran, and surrounding regions
3. **Arms and Armor** - European, Japanese, and Islamic arms and armor
4. **Arts of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas** - Traditional art from these regions
5. **Asian Art** - Comprehensive collection including Chinese, Japanese, Korean, South Asian, and Southeast Asian works
6. **Egyptian Art** - One of the finest collections outside Cairo, including the Temple of Dendur
7. **European Paintings** - Masterpieces from the 13th through early 20th centuries, including works by Rembrandt, Vermeer, and Van Gogh
8. **European Sculpture and Decorative Arts** - Renaissance to early 20th-century decorative arts
9. **Greek and Roman Art** - Classical antiquities
10. **Islamic Art** - Works from Spain to India spanning 7th-19th centuries
11. **Medieval Art** - European medieval art including the Cloisters branch museum
12. **Modern and Contemporary Art** - Works from 1890 to present
13. **Musical Instruments** - Historical instruments from around the world
14. **Photographs** - One of the earliest and most comprehensive photography collections
15. **Prints and Drawings** - Works on paper from the Middle Ages to present
16. **Robert Lehman Collection** - Diverse private collection bequeathed to the museum
17. **Costume Institute** - Fashion and costume design (hosts the annual Met Gala)

The Met also operates The Met Cloisters in Fort Tryon Park, dedicated to medieval European art and architecture.
