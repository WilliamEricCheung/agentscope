# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: Overview

## History
The Metropolitan Museum of Art (the Met) was founded in 1870 by a group of Americans including philanthropists, artists, and businessmen with the goal of creating a national institution to inspire and educate the public. The museum first opened on February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue in New York City. It moved to its current location on Fifth Avenue along Central Park's eastern edge in 1880. The main building has expanded significantly since then and now measures almost a quarter-mile long with over 2 million square feet of floor space.

## Significance
- The Met is the largest art museum in the Americas and the fourth-largest museum in the world by floor area
- It is one of the most-visited museums in the United States and the fifth-most visited art museum globally
- The museum houses approximately 1.5 million works of art spanning 5,000 years of world culture
- Its collection is organized into 17 curatorial departments
- The Met operates two additional locations: The Cloisters (medieval European art) in Fort Tryon Park and will absorb the Neue Galerie (early 20th century Austrian and German art) in 2028

## Main Collections

### Geographically Designated Collections
- **Ancient Near Eastern Art**: Over 7,000 pieces from Sumerian, Hittite, Assyrian, Babylonian, and other cultures
- **Egyptian Art**: More than 26,000 objects including the iconic Temple of Dendur and William the Faience Hippopotamus
- **Greek and Roman Art**: Over 17,000 objects including the Monteleone chariot and Cycladic sculptures
- **Asian Art**: More than 35,000 pieces spanning 4,000 years, with strengths in Chinese, Indian, Cambodian, and Japanese art
- **Islamic Art**: One of the world's largest collections with 12,000 works from Spain to Central Asia
- **Arts of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas**: Over 11,000 pieces housed in the Michael C. Rockefeller Wing

### European Collections
- **European Paintings**: Over 2,600 works from the 13th through early 20th centuries, including masterpieces by Rembrandt, Vermeer, and Van Gogh
- **Impressionist and Post-Impressionist**: World-class collection featuring works by Monet, Degas, Cézanne, and Van Gogh
- **European Sculpture and Decorative Arts**: Over 50,000 pieces including period rooms and the Vélez Blanco patio

### American Collections
- **American Wing**: Comprehensive collection of American art from the 18th through early 20th centuries
- **Art of Native America**: First displayed in the American Wing in 2018

### Specialized Collections
- **Arms and Armor**: 14,000 objects spanning cultures and time periods, including armor belonging to European royalty
- **Costume Institute**: Over 35,000 costumes and accessories, famous for the annual Met Gala
- **Musical Instruments**: 5,000 instruments from around the world, including Stradivari violins
- **Drawings and Prints**: 21,000 drawings and 1.2 million prints
- **Photographs**: Over 25,000 photographs including works by Stieglitz and Steichen
- **Modern and Contemporary Art**: 13,000 works including Picasso's portrait of Gertrude Stein and Pollock's Autumn Rhythm

### The Cloisters
A separate building dedicated solely to medieval European art, featuring the famous Hunt of the Unicorn tapestries and the Belles Heures illuminated manuscript.

The Met continues to be a cultural landmark that brings together artistic traditions from around the world, making it one of the most comprehensive and significant art museums globally.
