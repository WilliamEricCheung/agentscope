# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: Key Information Summary

## History
- Founded in 1870 by a group of American philanthropists, artists, and businessmen with the goal of creating a national institution to inspire and educate the public
- Officially incorporated on April 13, 1870, by the New York State Legislature
- First opened on February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue
- Moved to its current location at 1000 Fifth Avenue in 1880
- Notable founders included John Taylor Johnston (first president), George Palmer Putnam (founding superintendent), Eastman Johnson, Frederic Edwin Church, and Henry Gurdon Marquand
- Luigi Palma di Cesnola served as the first director from 1879 to 1904
- The museum's initial collection consisted of a Roman stone sarcophagus and 174 mostly European paintings

## Significance
- By floor area, it is the fourth-largest museum in the world and the largest art museum in the Americas
- One of the most-visited museums globally, with 5,727,258 visitors in fiscal year 2025
- Ranked as the most-visited museum in the United States and the fifth-most visited art museum in the world
- Houses approximately 1.5 million works of art (down from over 2 million in 2000)
- Collection is organized into 17 curatorial departments
- Main building spans approximately 2 million square feet (190,000 m²)
- Designated as a National Historic Landmark in 1986
- Operates three locations: the main building on Fifth Avenue, The Cloisters in Fort Tryon Park, and will merge with the Neue Galerie New York in 2028

## Main Collections

### Geographically Designated Collections
1. **Ancient Near Eastern Art**: Over 7,000 pieces spanning from the Neolithic Period through the fall of the Sasanian Empire, including works from Sumerian, Hittite, Assyrian, Babylonian, and Elamite cultures

2. **Arts of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas**: Over 11,000 pieces housed in the Michael C. Rockefeller Wing, featuring works from sub-Saharan Africa, the Pacific Islands, and the Americas dating from 3,000 BCE to present

3. **Asian Art**: More than 35,000 pieces representing 4,000 years of Asian art, with comprehensive collections of Chinese, Indian, Cambodian, Japanese, Korean, and Southeast Asian art

4. **Egyptian Art**: Over 26,000 objects from the Paleolithic era through the Ptolemaic era, displayed in 40 Egyptian galleries; highlights include the Temple of Dendur and wooden models from the Middle Kingdom

5. **European Paintings**: Over 2,600 works from the 13th through early 20th century, including significant collections of Old Masters, Impressionist, and Post-Impressionist works

6. **Greek and Roman Art**: More than 17,000 objects including the Monteleone chariot and Cycladic sculptures

7. **Islamic Art**: 12,000 works spanning from Spain to Central Asia, including miniature paintings, ceramics, textiles, and the reconstructed Nur Al-Din Room from Damascus

### Non-Geographically Designated Collections
1. **American Wing**: Comprehensive collection of American art from the 18th through early 20th century, including the Art of Native America exhibition

2. **Arms and Armor**: 14,000 objects spanning cultures from prehistoric times to the 20th century, including armor belonging to European royalty

3. **Costume Institute**: Over 35,000 costumes and accessories, known for hosting the annual Met Gala

4. **Drawings and Prints**: Approximately 21,000 drawings, 1.2 million prints, and 12,000 illustrated books

5. **Robert Lehman Collection**: 2,600 works donated in 1969, considered "one of the most extraordinary private art collections ever assembled in the United States"

6. **Medieval Art and The Cloisters**: Over 10,000 objects, with The Cloisters dedicated exclusively to medieval European art

7. **Modern and Contemporary Art**: 13,000 artworks including iconic works by Picasso, Pollock, and Johns

8. **Musical Instruments**: 5,000 instruments from around the world, including Stradivari violins and the oldest surviving piano (1720)

9. **Photographs**: Over 25,000 photographs from major collections including Alfred Stieglitz and Walker Evans

The Met's encyclopedic collection spans 5,000 years of world culture, from prehistory to the present, and represents virtually every part of the globe.
