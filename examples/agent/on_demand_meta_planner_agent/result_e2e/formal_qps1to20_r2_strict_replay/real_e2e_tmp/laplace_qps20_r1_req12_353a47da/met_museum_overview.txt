# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: A Concise Overview

## History
The Metropolitan Museum of Art (the Met) was founded in 1870 when the New York State Legislature granted it an Act of Incorporation "for the purpose of establishing and maintaining in said City a Museum and Library of Art." The museum was established by a group of Americans including philanthropists, artists, and businessmen who wanted to create a national institution that would inspire and educate the public.

Key historical milestones:
- Opened on February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue
- Moved to its current location at 1000 Fifth Avenue in 1880
- Founded by notable figures including John Taylor Johnston (first president), George Palmer Putnam (founding superintendent), and artists Eastman Johnson and Frederic Edwin Church
- Theodore Roosevelt Sr. (father of President Theodore Roosevelt) was among the founders
- Luigi Palma di Cesnola served as its first director from 1879 to 1904

## Significance
- Fourth-largest museum in the world by floor area
- Largest art museum in the Americas
- Most-visited museum in the United States (5.7 million visitors in fiscal year 2025)
- Fifth-most visited art museum in the world
- Houses approximately 1.5 million works of art
- Organized into 17 curatorial departments
- Main building spans approximately 2 million square feet
- Designated a National Historic Landmark in 1986

## Main Collections

### Geographically Designated Collections
1. **Ancient Near Eastern Art**: Over 7,000 pieces from Sumerian, Hittite, Assyrian, Babylonian, and other cultures
2. **Arts of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas**: More than 11,000 pieces housed in the Michael C. Rockefeller Wing
3. **Asian Art**: Over 35,000 pieces spanning 4,000 years, including Chinese, Indian, Cambodian, and Japanese works
4. **Egyptian Art**: More than 26,000 pieces from the Paleolithic era through the Ptolemaic era, including the famous Temple of Dendur
5. **European Paintings**: Over 2,600 works from the 13th through early 20th centuries, including masterpieces by Rembrandt, Vermeer, and many Impressionist and Post-Impressionist artists
6. **Greek and Roman Art**: More than 17,000 objects including the Monteleone chariot and Cycladic sculptures
7. **Islamic Art**: 12,000 pieces of art from Islamic cultures ranging from Spain to Central Asia

### Non-Geographically Designated Collections
1. **American Wing**: American art from the 18th through early 20th century
2. **Arms and Armor**: 14,000 objects spanning cultures and time periods, including armor belonging to European royalty
3. **Costume Institute**: Over 35,000 costumes and accessories, known for the annual Met Gala
4. **Drawings and Prints**: About 21,000 drawings and 1.2 million prints
5. **Robert Lehman Collection**: 2,600 works considered "one of the most extraordinary private art collections ever assembled in the United States"
6. **Medieval Art**: Over 10,000 objects split between the main building and The Cloisters
7. **Modern and Contemporary Art**: 13,000 artworks including iconic works by Picasso, Pollock, and Johns
8. **Musical Instruments**: 5,000 instruments from around the world, including Stradivari violins
9. **Photographs**: Over 25,000 photographs from major collections including Alfred Stieglitz's donation

### Additional Locations
- **The Cloisters**: Located in Fort Tryon Park, dedicated solely to medieval art
- **Met Breuer** (2016-2020): Formerly housed modern and contemporary art
- **Neue Galerie** (merging in 2028): Will become the Met's newest location focusing on early 20th century Austrian and German art
