# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: Overview

## History and Founding
The Metropolitan Museum of Art (the Met) was founded in 1870 when the New York State Legislature granted it an Act of Incorporation "for the purpose of establishing and maintaining in said City a Museum and Library of Art, of encouraging and developing the Study of the Fine Arts." The museum was established by a group of Americans, including philanthropists, artists, and businessmen, with the goal of creating a national institution that would inspire and educate the public.

Key founders included:
- John Taylor Johnston (railroad executive and first president)
- George Palmer Putnam (founding superintendent)
- Eastman Johnson and Frederic Edwin Church (artists and co-founders)
- Theodore Roosevelt Sr. (father of President Theodore Roosevelt)
- Henry Gurdon Marquand (major donor of Old Masters paintings)

The museum first opened on February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue. It moved to its current location at 1000 Fifth Avenue in 1880. The first portion of the building was constructed in 1880, and the museum has since expanded significantly to become one of the world's largest art museums.

## Significance
- By floor area, the Met is the fourth-largest museum in the world and the largest art museum in the Americas
- It was the most-visited museum in the United States with 5,727,258 visitors in fiscal year 2025
- It ranks as the fifth-most visited art museum in the world
- The museum's main building was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1986

## Main Collections
The Met's collection currently lists approximately 1.5 million works divided into 17 curatorial departments:

### Geographically Designated Collections
- **Ancient Near Eastern Art**: Over 7,000 pieces from Sumerian, Hittite, Assyrian, Babylonian, and other cultures
- **Arts of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas**: More than 11,000 pieces housed in the Michael C. Rockefeller Wing
- **Asian Art**: Over 35,000 pieces spanning 4,000 years, including comprehensive collections of Chinese, Indian, and Cambodian art
- **Egyptian Art**: More than 26,000 pieces from the Paleolithic era through the Ptolemaic era, featuring the iconic Temple of Dendur
- **European Paintings**: Over 2,600 works from the 13th through early 20th century, including significant Impressionist and Post-Impressionist collections
- **Greek and Roman Art**: More than 17,000 objects, including the Monteleone chariot and Cycladic sculptures
- **Islamic Art**: One of the world's largest collections of Islamic art with approximately 12,000 objects

### Non-Geographically Designated Collections
- **American Wing**: Comprehensive collection of American art from the 18th through early 20th century
- **Arms and Armor**: 14,000 objects spanning cultures from ancient Egypt to 20th-century America
- **Costume Institute**: Over 35,000 costumes and accessories, known for the annual Met Gala
- **Drawings and Prints**: About 21,000 drawings and 1.2 million prints
- **Robert Lehman Collection**: 2,600 works considered "one of the most extraordinary private art collections ever assembled in the United States"
- **Medieval Art**: Over 10,000 objects split between the main building and The Cloisters
- **Modern and Contemporary Art**: 13,000 artworks including iconic works by Picasso, Pollock, and Johns
- **Musical Instruments**: 5,000 instruments from around the world, including Stradivari violins
- **Photographs**: Over 25,000 photographs from major collections including Alfred Stieglitz's donation

## Additional Locations
- **The Cloisters**: Located in Fort Tryon Park, dedicated to medieval European art, completed in 1938
- **Neue Galerie**: Set to become the museum's newest location in 2028 following a merger, focusing on early 20th-century Austrian and German art
