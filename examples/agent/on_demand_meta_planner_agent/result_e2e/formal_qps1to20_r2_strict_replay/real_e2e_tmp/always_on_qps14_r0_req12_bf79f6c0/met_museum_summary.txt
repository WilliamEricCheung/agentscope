# Metropolitan Museum of Art: Key Information Summary

## History
- **Founded**: April 13, 1870, by the New York State Legislature through an Act of Incorporation
- **First opened**: February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue
- **Founders**: A group of American philanthropists, artists, and businessmen including Theodore Roosevelt Sr. (father of President Theodore Roosevelt), John Taylor Johnston (railroad executive), Eastman Johnson (artist), and Henry Gurdon Marquand
- **First director**: Luigi Palma di Cesnola (served 1879-1904)
- **Moved to current location**: 1880
- **Original collection**: Started with a Roman stone sarcophagus and 174 mostly European paintings

## Location
- **Main building**: 1000 Fifth Avenue, along Museum Mile on the eastern edge of Central Park in Manhattan's Upper East Side, New York City
- **Second location**: The Cloisters at Fort Tryon Park in Upper Manhattan (dedicated to medieval European art, architecture, and artifacts)
- **Future third location**: Neue Galerie New York (set to merge with the Met in 2028, focusing on early 20th century Austrian and German art)
- **Building size**: Over 2 million square feet (190,000 m²), making it the fourth-largest museum in the world by floor area and the largest art museum in the Americas
- **Architecture**: The original "mausoleum" was designed by Calvert Vaux and Jacob Wrey Mould; the Fifth Avenue facade, Great Hall, and Grand Stairway were designed in Beaux-Arts style by Richard Morris Hunt

## Notable Features and Collections
- **Collection size**: Currently houses approximately 1.5 million works of art (down from over 2 million in 2000)
- **Organization**: Divided into 17 curatorial departments with specialized staff
- **Visitor statistics**: Most-visited museum in the United States with 5,727,258 visitors in fiscal year 2025; fifth-most visited art museum in the world

### Major Collection Highlights:
1. **Egyptian Art**: Over 26,000 pieces including the famous Temple of Dendur, wooden models from Middle Kingdom tombs, and William the Faience Hippopotamus (the museum's informal mascot)

2. **European Paintings**: Over 2,600 works from the 13th through early 20th centuries, including masterpieces by Old Masters, Impressionists, and Post-Impressionists

3. **American Wing**: Comprehensive collection of American art from the 18th through early 20th century, including Indigenous American art since 2018

4. **Arts of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas**: Housed in the Michael C. Rockefeller Wing, containing over 11,000 pieces from sub-Saharan Africa, the Pacific Islands, and the Americas

5. **Asian Art**: One of the most comprehensive collections in the US with over 35,000 pieces spanning 4,000 years, including the Astor Court (a complete Ming Dynasty-style garden court)

6. **Islamic Art**: One of the world's largest collections with 12,000 objects from Spain to Central Asia, including the reconstructed Nur Al-Din Room from Damascus

7. **Greek and Roman Art**: Over 17,000 objects including the Monteleone chariot and reconstructed rooms from ancient villas

8. **Arms and Armor**: One of the museum's most popular collections with 14,000 objects spanning from prehistoric times to the 20th century

9. **Costume Institute**: Over 35,000 costumes and accessories, famous for hosting the annual Met Gala

10. **Medieval Art**: Split between the main building and The Cloisters, featuring items like the Hunt of the Unicorn tapestries

11. **Modern and Contemporary Art**: Includes iconic works like Picasso's portrait of Gertrude Stein and Jackson Pollock's Autumn Rhythm

12. **Special Collections**: Musical instruments (5,000 examples including Stradivari violins), photographs (25,000+), drawings and prints (1.2 million prints, 21,000 drawings)

The Met is renowned not just for its vast collection but also for its period rooms that recreate historical interiors from 1st-century Rome through modern American design, allowing visitors to experience art in context.
