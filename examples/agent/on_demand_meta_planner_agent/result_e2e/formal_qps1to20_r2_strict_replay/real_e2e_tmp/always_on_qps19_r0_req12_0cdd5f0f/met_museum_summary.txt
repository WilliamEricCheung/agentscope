# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: Key Information Summary

## History and Founding
- Founded in 1870 by the New York State Legislature with an Act of Incorporation
- Officially opened on February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue
- Established by a group of American philanthropists, artists, and businessmen including Theodore Roosevelt Sr. (father of President Theodore Roosevelt), John Taylor Johnston (first president), and Henry Gurdon Marquand
- Moved to its current location at 1000 Fifth Avenue in 1880
- The museum's mission was to create a national institution that would inspire and educate the public through art

## Significance and Scale
- Colloquially known as "The Met"
- Fourth-largest museum in the world by floor area and the largest art museum in the Americas
- Most-visited museum in the United States with over 5.7 million visitors annually (as of fiscal year 2025)
- Fifth-most visited art museum globally
- Houses approximately 1.5 million works of art (down from over 2 million in 2000)
- Collection divided into 17 curatorial departments
- Main building spans over 2 million square feet (190,000 m²)
- Designated a National Historic Landmark in 1986

## Main Collections and Departments

### Geographically Designated Collections:
1. **Ancient Near Eastern Art** - Over 7,000 pieces from Sumerian, Hittite, Assyrian, Babylonian, and other cultures
2. **Arts of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas** - Over 11,000 pieces housed in the Michael C. Rockefeller Wing, including indigenous Australian rock paintings and Benin Court artifacts
3. **Asian Art** - Over 35,000 pieces spanning 4,000 years, including comprehensive collections of Chinese, Indian, Cambodian, and Japanese art
4. **Egyptian Art** - Over 26,000 pieces from Paleolithic era through Ptolemaic era, featuring the famous Temple of Dendur as centerpiece
5. **European Paintings** - Over 2,600 works from 13th through early 20th century, including Old Masters, Impressionist, and Post-Impressionist works
6. **Greek and Roman Art** - Over 17,000 objects including the Monteleone chariot and Cycladic sculptures
7. **Islamic Art** - 12,000+ works from Spain to Central Asia, including miniature paintings and calligraphy

### Non-Geographically Designated Collections:
1. **American Wing** - American art from 18th through early 20th century
2. **Arms and Armor** - 14,000 objects spanning global cultures from prehistoric times to modern era
3. **Costume Institute** - Over 35,000 costumes and accessories, famous for the annual Met Gala
4. **Drawings and Prints** - 21,000 drawings, 1.2 million prints, and 12,000 illustrated books
5. **Robert Lehman Collection** - 2,600 works donated in 1969, considered one of the most extraordinary private collections ever assembled in the US
6. **Medieval Art and The Cloisters** - Over 10,000 objects, with The Cloisters location dedicated solely to medieval European art
7. **Modern and Contemporary Art** - 13,000 artworks including iconic works by Picasso, Pollock, and Johns
8. **Musical Instruments** - 5,000 instruments from around the world, including Stradivari violins and the oldest surviving piano (1720)
9. **Photographs** - Over 25,000 photographs from major collections including Alfred Stieglitz and Walker Evans

## Additional Locations
- **The Cloisters** (Fort Tryon Park, Upper Manhattan) - Dedicated to medieval European art, completed in 1938
- **Met Breuer** (2016-2020) - Formerly operated at 945 Madison Avenue for modern and contemporary art
- **Neue Galerie** (planned merger in 2028) - Will become the Met's third location focusing on early 20th century Austrian and German art

## Cultural Impact
- Hosts the famous annual Met Gala through the Costume Institute
- Maintains extensive digital collections with over 375,000 images available under Creative Commons Zero
- Has faced controversies regarding looted art and repatriation issues, leading to recent policy changes and returns of artifacts to source countries
- Continues to be a major cultural and educational institution serving millions of visitors annually
