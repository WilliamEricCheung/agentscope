# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: Key Information

## History
The Metropolitan Museum of Art (the Met) was founded in 1870 when the New York State Legislature granted it an Act of Incorporation. The museum was established by a group of Americans, including philanthropists, artists, and businessmen, with the goal of creating a national institution that would inspire and educate the public. It first opened to the public on February 20, 1872, in a building at 681 Fifth Avenue. The founders included notable figures such as Theodore Roosevelt Sr. (father of President Theodore Roosevelt), railroad executive John Taylor Johnston (who served as its first president), and artists Eastman Johnson and Frederic Edwin Church. The museum moved to its current location in 1880.

## Location
The main building of the Metropolitan Museum of Art is located at 1000 Fifth Avenue, along Museum Mile on the eastern edge of Central Park on Manhattan's Upper East Side in New York City. After negotiations with the City of New York in 1871, the Met was granted the land between East Park Drive, Fifth Avenue, and the 79th and 85th Street transverse roads in Central Park. The first portion of the approximately 2-million-square-foot building was constructed in 1880. The museum has since expanded significantly and now measures almost 1/4-mile long with more than 2 million square feet of floor space.

The Met also operates a second location called The Cloisters at Fort Tryon Park in Upper Manhattan, which houses an extensive collection of medieval European art, architecture, and artifacts. Additionally, the museum announced in 2026 that it will merge with the nearby Neue Galerie New York in 2028, which will become its third location dedicated to early 20th century Austrian and German art.

## Significance
The Metropolitan Museum of Art is one of the world's most significant cultural institutions:
- It is the fourth-largest museum in the world by floor area and the largest art museum in the Americas
- It was the most-visited museum in the United States and the fifth-most visited art museum globally, with over 5.7 million visitors in fiscal year 2025
- Its permanent collection contains approximately 1.5 million works of art spanning 5,000 years of world culture
- The collection is divided into 17 curatorial departments covering diverse areas including ancient Near Eastern art, Egyptian art, Greek and Roman art, European paintings, American art, Asian art, Islamic art, African art, and modern and contemporary art
- The museum houses iconic works such as the Temple of Dendur, the Monteleone chariot, and masterpieces by artists like Picasso, Van Gogh, and Rembrandt
- It maintains encyclopedic collections of musical instruments, costumes, decorative arts, and antique weapons and armor from around the world

The Met's significance extends beyond its vast collection; it has played a crucial role in art education, conservation, and scholarship since its founding, making art accessible to millions of visitors annually.
