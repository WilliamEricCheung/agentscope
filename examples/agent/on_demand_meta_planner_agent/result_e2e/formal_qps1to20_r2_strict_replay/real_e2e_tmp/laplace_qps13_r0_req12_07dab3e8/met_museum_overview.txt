# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: Overview

## History
The Metropolitan Museum of Art (the Met) was founded in 1870 by a group of American philanthropists, artists, and businessmen with the goal of creating a national institution to inspire and educate the public. The museum first opened on February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue, before moving to its current location in 1880. The founders included notable figures such as Theodore Roosevelt Sr. (father of President Theodore Roosevelt), railroad executive John Taylor Johnston, and artist Eastman Johnson. Luigi Palma di Cesnola served as the museum's first director from 1879 to 1904.

## Significance
The Met is one of the world's most important cultural institutions:
- It is the largest art museum in the Americas and the fourth-largest museum in the world by floor area
- With approximately 5.7 million visitors annually (as of fiscal year 2025), it is the most-visited museum in the United States and the fifth-most visited art museum globally
- The museum houses approximately 1.5 million works of art spanning 5,000 years of world culture
- Its collection is organized into 17 curatorial departments
- The Met has been designated a National Historic Landmark (1986) for both its monumental architecture and cultural importance

## Location
The main building is located at 1000 Fifth Avenue, along Museum Mile on the eastern edge of Central Park on Manhattan's Upper East Side in New York City. The approximately 2-million-square-foot building was constructed in phases beginning in 1880.

The Met also operates two additional locations:
- The Cloisters at Fort Tryon Park in Upper Manhattan, which houses an extensive collection of medieval European art, architecture, and artifacts
- The Met Breuer (operated 2016-2020) was a modern and contemporary art gallery at 945 Madison Avenue
- A third site, currently the Neue Galerie New York, is set to become the Met's newest location in 2028 when the museum merges with and absorbs the smaller Neue Galerie and its collection of early 20th century Austrian and German art

## Main Collections
The Met's encyclopedic collection spans cultures and time periods:

### Ancient Art
- **Egyptian Art**: Over 26,000 pieces from the Paleolithic era through the Ptolemaic era, including the famous Temple of Dendur
- **Greek and Roman Art**: More than 17,000 objects including sculptures, vases, and the reconstructed bedroom from a villa in Boscoreale
- **Ancient Near Eastern Art**: Over 7,000 pieces from Sumerian, Hittite, Assyrian, Babylonian, and other cultures

### European Art
- **European Paintings**: Over 2,600 works from the 13th through early 20th centuries, including masterpieces by Rembrandt, Vermeer, and many Old Masters
- **Impressionist and Post-Impressionist**: One of the world's great repositories featuring works by Monet, Degas, Van Gogh, Cézanne, and others
- **European Sculpture and Decorative Arts**: Over 50,000 pieces from the 15th through early 20th centuries, including period rooms and the Vélez Blanco patio

### American Art
- **American Wing**: Comprehensive collection of American art from the 18th through early 20th century
- **Art of Native America**: First appeared in the American Wing in 2018, featuring Indigenous American art

### Global Collections
- **Asian Art**: Over 35,000 pieces spanning 4,000 years, including the Astor Court (a Ming Dynasty-style garden court)
- **Arts of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas**: Over 11,000 pieces housed in the Michael C. Rockefeller Wing
- **Islamic Art**: One of the world's largest collections with 12,000 works from Spain to Central Asia

### Specialized Collections
- **Arms and Armor**: 14,000 objects including armor belonging to European royalty
- **Costume Institute**: Over 35,000 costumes and accessories, known for the annual Met Gala
- **Musical Instruments**: Approximately 5,000 instruments from around the world, including Stradivari violins
- **Drawings and Prints**: About 21,000 drawings and 1.2 million prints
- **Photographs**: Over 25,000 photographs including works by Stieglitz, Steichen, and Evans
- **Modern and Contemporary Art**: 13,000 works including Picasso's portrait of Gertrude Stein and Pollock's Autumn Rhythm

The Met continues to be a vital cultural institution that preserves, studies, and presents art from across the globe and throughout human history.
