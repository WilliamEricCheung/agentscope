# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: Overview

## History and Founding
The Metropolitan Museum of Art (the Met) was founded in 1870 by a group of Americans including philanthropists, artists, and businessmen with the goal of creating a national institution to inspire and educate the public. The museum first opened on February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue, before moving to its current location at 1000 Fifth Avenue in 1880. Key founders included John Taylor Johnston (railroad executive and first president), George Palmer Putnam (founding superintendent), and artists Eastman Johnson and Frederic Edwin Church. Theodore Roosevelt Sr., father of President Theodore Roosevelt, was also among the founders.

## Significance
- By floor area, the Met is the fourth-largest museum in the world and the largest art museum in the Americas
- It is the most-visited museum in the United States and the fifth-most visited art museum globally
- The main building spans approximately 2 million square feet (190,000 m²)
- The museum's collection has grown from an initial holding of a Roman sarcophagus and 174 European paintings to over 1.5 million works

## Main Collections
The Met's encyclopedic collection is divided into 17 curatorial departments:

1. **European Paintings** - Includes Old Masters, Impressionist, and Post-Impressionist works with strengths in artists like Rembrandt, Vermeer, Monet, Van Gogh, and Cézanne

2. **American Wing** - Features American art from the 18th through early 20th century, including paintings, sculpture, and decorative arts

3. **Egyptian Art** - Over 26,000 pieces spanning from the Paleolithic era through the Ptolemaic era, with the Temple of Dendur as its centerpiece

4. **Greek and Roman Art** - More than 17,000 objects including the Monteleone chariot and Cycladic sculptures

5. **Asian Art** - Over 35,000 pieces spanning 4,000 years, with comprehensive collections of Chinese, Indian, Cambodian, and Japanese art

6. **Arts of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas** - Housed in the Michael C. Rockefeller Wing with over 11,000 pieces

7. **Islamic Art** - One of the world's largest collections with 12,000 works from Spain to Central Asia

8. **Modern and Contemporary Art** - Includes iconic works by Picasso, Pollock, Johns, and Beckmann

9. **Arms and Armor** - One of the museum's most popular collections with 14,000 objects spanning global cultures

10. **Costume Institute** - Over 35,000 costumes and accessories, known for the annual Met Gala

11. **Musical Instruments** - About 5,000 instruments from around the world, including Stradivari violins

12. **Drawings and Prints** - Approximately 21,000 drawings and 1.2 million prints

13. **Photographs** - Over 25,000 photographs from major collections including Stieglitz and Evans

14. **European Sculpture and Decorative Arts** - Over 50,000 pieces including period rooms and Renaissance sculpture

15. **Medieval Art** - Split between the main building and The Cloisters, with over 10,000 objects

16. **Ancient Near Eastern Art** - Over 7,000 pieces from Sumerian, Assyrian, Babylonian, and other cultures

17. **Robert Lehman Collection** - A "museum within the museum" with 2,600 works reflecting Lehman's personal collecting interests

## Additional Locations
- **The Cloisters**: Located in Fort Tryon Park, dedicated solely to medieval European art
- **Met Breuer** (2016-2020): Formerly housed modern and contemporary art in the former Whitney Museum building
- **Neue Galerie** (planned merger in 2028): Will become the Met's third location focusing on early 20th century Austrian and German art
