# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: Key Information

## Overview
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, colloquially known as "the Met," is an encyclopedic art museum in New York City. It is the fourth-largest museum in the world by floor area and the largest art museum in the Americas. With over 5.7 million visitors in fiscal year 2025, it was the most-visited museum in the United States and the fifth-most visited art museum globally.

## History
The Metropolitan Museum of Art was founded in 1870 when the New York State Legislature granted it an Act of Incorporation "for the purpose of establishing and maintaining in said City a Museum and Library of Art, of encouraging and developing the Study of the Fine Arts, and the application of Art to manufacture and natural life, of advancing the general knowledge of kindred subjects, and to that end of furnishing popular instruction and recreations."

The museum was established by a group of Americans including philanthropists, artists, and businessmen who wanted to create a national institution that would inspire and educate the public. Key founders included:
- John Taylor Johnston (railroad executive and first president)
- George Palmer Putnam (founding superintendent)
- Eastman Johnson (artist and co-founder)
- Frederic Edwin Church (landscape painter and co-founder)
- Theodore Roosevelt Sr. (father of President Theodore Roosevelt)
- Henry Gurdon Marquand (major donor of Old Masters paintings)

The museum first opened on February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue. It moved to its current location in 1880 after outgrowing its initial spaces.

## Location
The main building is located at 1000 Fifth Avenue, along Museum Mile on the eastern edge of Central Park on Manhattan's Upper East Side. This building spans approximately 2 million square feet (190,000 m²), making it one of the world's largest art museums.

The Met has two additional locations:
1. The Cloisters at Fort Tryon Park in Upper Manhattan, which houses medieval European art, architecture, and artifacts
2. The Neue Galerie New York, which will become the Met's third location in 2028 following a planned merger

## Collection and Significance
The Met's permanent collection contains approximately 1.5 million works of art divided into 17 curatorial departments. The collection spans 5,000 years of world culture, from prehistory to the present, and includes:

- Ancient Near Eastern and Egyptian art
- Classical antiquities from Greece and Rome
- European paintings and sculptures from the Old Masters through the 19th century
- American art from the 18th through early 20th centuries
- Modern and contemporary art
- Extensive holdings of African, Asian, Oceanian, Byzantine, and Islamic art
- Encyclopedic collections of musical instruments, costumes, decorative arts, textiles, and antique weapons and armor

The museum is particularly renowned for iconic pieces such as the Temple of Dendur (Egyptian art), the extensive Impressionist and Post-Impressionist collection, and the medieval art at The Cloisters.

As one of the world's premier cultural institutions, the Met serves as both a repository of global artistic heritage and an educational resource, fulfilling its founders' vision of bringing art and art education to the American people.
