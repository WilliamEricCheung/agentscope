# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: Concise Overview

## History
The Metropolitan Museum of Art (the Met) was founded in 1870 by a group of American philanthropists, artists, and businessmen with the goal of creating a national institution to inspire and educate the public. The museum first opened on February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue before moving to its current location in 1880. The founders included notable figures such as Theodore Roosevelt Sr. (father of President Theodore Roosevelt), railroad executive John Taylor Johnston, and artist Eastman Johnson. Luigi Palma di Cesnola, a former Civil War officer, served as the museum's first director from 1879 to 1904.

## Significance
- The Met is the fourth-largest museum in the world by floor area and the largest art museum in the Americas
- It is the most-visited museum in the United States and the fifth-most visited art museum globally, with over 5.7 million visitors in fiscal year 2025
- The museum houses approximately 1.5 million works of art spanning 5,000 years of world culture
- Its collection is organized into 17 curatorial departments
- The Met has played a crucial role in bringing art and art education to the American public since its founding

## Location
- Main Building: 1000 Fifth Avenue, along Museum Mile on the eastern edge of Central Park in Manhattan's Upper East Side
- The main building covers approximately 2 million square feet (190,000 m²)
- The Cloisters: A second location at Fort Tryon Park in Upper Manhattan dedicated to medieval European art, architecture, and artifacts
- Future Third Site: The Met will merge with the Neue Galerie New York in 2028, adding a collection of early 20th-century Austrian and German art

## Main Collections
The Met's encyclopedic collection spans cultures and time periods:

### Geographically Designated Collections
- **Ancient Near Eastern Art**: Over 7,000 pieces from Sumerian, Hittite, Assyrian, Babylonian, and other cultures
- **Egyptian Art**: More than 26,000 objects including the famous Temple of Dendur and William the Faience Hippopotamus (the museum's informal mascot)
- **Greek and Roman Art**: Over 17,000 objects including the Monteleone chariot and Cycladic sculptures
- **Asian Art**: More than 35,000 pieces spanning 4,000 years, with comprehensive collections of Chinese, Indian, and Cambodian art
- **Islamic Art**: One of the world's largest collections with 12,000 works from Spain to Central Asia
- **Arts of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas**: Over 11,000 pieces housed in the Michael C. Rockefeller Wing

### European Collections
- **European Paintings**: Over 2,600 works from the 13th through early 20th centuries, including significant Impressionist and Post-Impressionist works
- **European Sculpture and Decorative Arts**: Over 50,000 pieces from the 15th through early 20th centuries, including period rooms and Renaissance sculpture

### American Collections
- **American Wing**: Comprehensive collection of American art from the 18th through early 20th centuries
- **Art of Native America**: Indigenous American art displayed since 2018

### Specialized Collections
- **Arms and Armor**: 14,000 objects spanning global cultures and time periods
- **Costume Institute**: Over 35,000 costumes and accessories, known for the annual Met Gala
- **Musical Instruments**: About 5,000 instruments from around the world, including Stradivari violins
- **Photographs**: Over 25,000 photographs from major collections
- **Modern and Contemporary Art**: 13,000 works including iconic pieces by Picasso, Pollock, and Johns
- **Robert Lehman Collection**: One of the most extraordinary private collections ever assembled in the US

The Met continues to be a cultural landmark that brings together artistic traditions from around the world, making it one of the most comprehensive and significant art museums globally.
