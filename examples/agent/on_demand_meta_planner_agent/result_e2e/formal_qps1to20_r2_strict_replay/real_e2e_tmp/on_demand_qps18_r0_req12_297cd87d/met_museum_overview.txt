# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: Overview

## History
The Metropolitan Museum of Art (often referred to as "The Met") was founded in 1870 by a group of American citizens including businessmen, financiers, leading artists, and thinkers. The museum opened to the public on February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue in New York City. In 1880, it moved to its current location in Central Park along what is now known as Museum Mile on Fifth Avenue.

The museum's iconic Beaux-Arts facade was designed by architect Richard Morris Hunt and completed in 1902. Over the decades, the museum has undergone numerous expansions, including the addition of The Met Cloisters in 1938 (dedicated to medieval art) and The Met Breuer (formerly the Whitney Museum building) which operated as a branch from 2016 to 2020.

## Significance
The Met is one of the world's largest and most prestigious art museums. It is:
- The largest art museum in the United States
- Among the most visited museums globally, typically welcoming over 6 million visitors annually pre-pandemic
- A cultural landmark of New York City and the United States
- An encyclopedic museum with collections spanning 5,000 years of world culture
- A major center for art conservation, research, and education

The Met plays a crucial role in preserving cultural heritage and making art accessible to the public. Its annual Met Gala (formally the Costume Institute Benefit) is one of the most prominent fundraising events in the fashion world.

## Main Collections
The Met houses over 2 million works divided among 17 curatorial departments:

1. **American Wing** - American decorative arts, paintings, sculptures, and period rooms from the 18th through early 20th centuries

2. **Ancient Near Eastern Art** - Artifacts from Mesopotamia, Iran, and surrounding regions dating from 8,000 BCE to the 7th century CE

3. **Arms and Armor** - European, Japanese, and Islamic arms and armor from the 5th to the 19th century

4. **Arts of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas** - Works from sub-Saharan Africa, the Pacific Islands, and North, Central, and South America

5. **Asian Art** - One of the most comprehensive collections in the West, including Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Indian, and Southeast Asian art

6. **Costume Institute** - More than 35,000 costumes and accessories spanning five continents and centuries

7. **Egyptian Art** - One of the finest collections outside Cairo, including the Temple of Dendur

8. **European Paintings** - Masterpieces from the 13th through early 20th centuries, including works by Rembrandt, Vermeer, Monet, and Van Gogh

9. **European Sculpture and Decorative Arts** - Renaissance to early 20th-century sculpture, furniture, tapestries, and decorative objects

10. **Greek and Roman Art** - Classical antiquities from the Neolithic period through the late Roman Empire

11. **Islamic Art** - Works from Spain to India created from the 7th to the 21st century

12. **Medieval Art** - European art from the 12th to the 16th centuries, primarily housed at The Met Cloisters

13. **Modern and Contemporary Art** - Works from 1890 to the present

14. **Musical Instruments** - Over 5,000 instruments from six continents

15. **Photographs** - Images from the invention of photography to the present

16. **Prints and Drawings** - Works on paper from the 15th century to the present

17. **Robert Lehman Collection** - A distinguished private collection integrated into the museum

The Met continues to expand its collections and exhibitions, maintaining its mission to collect, preserve, study, and present significant works of art across time and cultures.
