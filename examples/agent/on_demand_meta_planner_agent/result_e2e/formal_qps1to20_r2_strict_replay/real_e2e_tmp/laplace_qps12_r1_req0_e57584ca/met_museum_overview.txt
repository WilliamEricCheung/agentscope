# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: Visitor Overview

## History
The Metropolitan Museum of Art (often called "the Met") was founded on April 13, 1870, by a group of American philanthropists, artists, and businessmen who wanted to create a national institution that would inspire and educate the public. The museum first opened to the public on February 20, 1872, in a building at 681 Fifth Avenue. It moved to its current location on Fifth Avenue in 1880. The founders included notable figures such as Theodore Roosevelt Sr. (father of President Theodore Roosevelt), railroad executive John Taylor Johnston, and artist Eastman Johnson.

## Significance
- The Met is the fourth-largest museum in the world by floor area and the largest art museum in the Americas
- It is the most-visited museum in the United States, with over 5.7 million visitors in fiscal year 2025
- The museum houses approximately 1.5 million works of art spanning 5,000 years of world culture
- Its collection is divided into 17 curatorial departments covering virtually every major artistic tradition

## Location
- Main Building: 1000 Fifth Avenue, along Museum Mile on the eastern edge of Central Park on Manhattan's Upper East Side
- Second Location: The Cloisters at Fort Tryon Park in Upper Manhattan, which houses an extensive collection of medieval European art, architecture, and artifacts
- Future Third Location: The Neue Galerie New York (set to become part of the Met in 2028)

## Key Facts for Visitors
- The main building spans approximately 2 million square feet (190,000 m²)
- Must-see highlights include:
  * The Temple of Dendur (Egyptian art)
  * European masterpieces by Rembrandt, Vermeer, and others
  * The American Wing with period rooms and sculptures
  * The Arms and Armor collection with its iconic mounted knights
  * The Costume Institute (home of the annual Met Gala)
  * The Asian Art collection featuring the Astor Court, a complete Ming Dynasty-style garden court
  * The medieval art at The Cloisters, including the famous "Hunt of the Unicorn" tapestries

- Admission policy: As of March 2018, out-of-state and foreign visitors pay $25, while New York State residents can pay what they wish (at least a nominal amount)
- The museum offers extensive educational programs, special exhibitions, and performances throughout the year
- The Met has made over 375,000 images of public-domain works available under Creative Commons Zero (CC0) through its Open Access initiative

The Metropolitan Museum of Art provides an unparalleled journey through human creativity across cultures and centuries, making it an essential destination for any visitor to New York City.
