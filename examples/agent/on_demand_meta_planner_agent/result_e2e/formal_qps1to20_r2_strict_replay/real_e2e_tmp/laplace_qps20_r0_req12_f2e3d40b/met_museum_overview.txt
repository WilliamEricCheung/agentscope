# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: Overview

## History
The Metropolitan Museum of Art (the Met) was founded in 1870 by a group of American philanthropists, artists, and businessmen with the goal of creating a national institution to inspire and educate the public. The museum first opened on February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue in New York City. It moved to its current location on Fifth Avenue along Central Park's eastern edge in 1880. The founders included notable figures such as Theodore Roosevelt Sr. (father of President Theodore Roosevelt), railroad executive John Taylor Johnston, and artist Eastman Johnson.

## Significance
- **Scale**: The Met is the fourth-largest museum in the world by floor area and the largest art museum in the Americas, with approximately 2 million square feet of space.
- **Collections**: Houses around 1.5 million works of art spanning 5,000 years of world culture.
- **Visitation**: One of the most-visited museums globally, attracting over 5.7 million visitors annually (as of fiscal year 2025).
- **Cultural Impact**: Serves as a cornerstone of American cultural education and international art scholarship.

## Main Collections
The Met's collection is organized into 17 curatorial departments:

### Geographically Designated Collections
- **Ancient Near Eastern Art**: Over 7,000 pieces from Sumerian, Assyrian, Babylonian, and other ancient civilizations
- **Egyptian Art**: More than 26,000 objects including the iconic Temple of Dendur and artifacts from the museum's own excavations (1906-1941)
- **Greek and Roman Art**: Over 17,000 objects including the Monteleone chariot and Cycladic sculptures
- **Asian Art**: More than 35,000 pieces spanning 4,000 years, featuring comprehensive collections of Chinese, Indian, Cambodian, and Japanese art
- **Islamic Art**: 12,000 works from Spain to Central Asia, including miniature paintings and calligraphy
- **Arts of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas**: Over 11,000 pieces housed in the Michael C. Rockefeller Wing, including works from sub-Saharan Africa, Pacific Islands, and indigenous American cultures

### European Collections
- **European Paintings**: Over 2,600 works from the 13th to early 20th centuries, including masterpieces by Rembrandt, Vermeer, and extensive Impressionist and Post-Impressionist collections
- **European Sculpture and Decorative Arts**: Over 50,000 pieces from the 15th to early 20th centuries, featuring period rooms and Renaissance sculpture
- **Robert Lehman Collection**: One of America's most extraordinary private collections, strong in Italian Renaissance and Northern European paintings

### American Collections
- **American Wing**: Comprehensive collection of American art from the 18th through early 20th centuries
- **Art of Native America**: Indigenous American art displayed since 2018

### Specialized Collections
- **Modern and Contemporary Art**: 13,000 works including iconic pieces by Picasso, Pollock, and Johns
- **Arms and Armor**: 14,000 objects spanning from prehistoric times to the 20th century
- **Costume Institute**: Over 35,000 costumes and accessories, famous for the annual Met Gala
- **Musical Instruments**: 5,000 instruments from around the world, including Stradivari violins
- **Photographs**: Over 25,000 images from major photography collections
- **Drawings and Prints**: 21,000 drawings and 1.2 million prints

### The Cloisters
A separate branch located in Fort Tryon Park, dedicated exclusively to medieval European art and architecture, featuring the famous "Hunt of the Unicorn" tapestries and incorporating five medieval French cloisters into its structure.

The Met continues to evolve as both a repository of global artistic heritage and a dynamic cultural institution, balancing its encyclopedic mission with contemporary challenges of provenance, accessibility, and representation.
