# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: Overview

## History
The Metropolitan Museum of Art (the Met) was founded in 1870 by a group of Americans including philanthropists, artists, and businessmen with the goal of creating a national institution to inspire and educate the public. The museum first opened on February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue, before moving to its current location in 1880. The founders included notable figures such as Theodore Roosevelt Sr. (father of President Theodore Roosevelt), railroad executive John Taylor Johnston, and artist Eastman Johnson.

## Location
The main building is located at 1000 Fifth Avenue, along Museum Mile on the eastern edge of Central Park on Manhattan's Upper East Side in New York City. The museum occupies more than 2 million square feet of floor space, making it one of the world's largest art museums by area. The Met also operates a second location called The Cloisters at Fort Tryon Park in Upper Manhattan, which houses an extensive collection of medieval European art, architecture, and artifacts. A third site, currently the Neue Galerie New York, is set to become the museum's newest location in 2028.

## Notable Features
- **Size and Scope**: The Met is the fourth-largest museum in the world by floor area and the largest art museum in the Americas. It houses approximately 1.5 million works divided into 17 curatorial departments.

- **Collections**: The museum's encyclopedic collection spans 5,000 years of world culture, from prehistory to the present, and includes:
  - Ancient Near Eastern, Egyptian, Greek, and Roman art
  - European paintings and sculpture from the Old Masters through Impressionism and Post-Impressionism
  - American art from the 18th through early 20th centuries
  - Extensive holdings of African, Asian, Oceanian, Byzantine, and Islamic art
  - Encyclopedic collections of musical instruments, costumes, decorative arts, textiles, and antique weapons and armor

- **Architectural Highlights**: The main building features Beaux-Arts architecture designed by Richard Morris Hunt, with later additions by McKim, Mead & White and modernistic glass elements by Roche-Dinkeloo.

- **Iconic Exhibits**: 
  - The Temple of Dendur, an ancient Egyptian temple gifted to the United States in 1965
  - The Michael C. Rockefeller Wing housing arts of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas
  - The Astor Court, a complete Ming Dynasty-style garden court
  - The Robert Lehman Collection, considered "one of the most extraordinary private art collections ever assembled in the United States"
  - The Costume Institute, known for its annual Met Gala and fashion exhibitions

- **Visitor Statistics**: The Met is the most-visited museum in the United States, attracting over 5.7 million visitors in fiscal year 2025, making it the fifth-most visited art museum in the world.
