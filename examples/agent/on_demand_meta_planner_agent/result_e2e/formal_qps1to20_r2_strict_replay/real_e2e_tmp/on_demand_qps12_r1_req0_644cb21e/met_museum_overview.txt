# The Metropolitan Museum of Art - Overview

## History
The Metropolitan Museum of Art (often referred to as "The Met") was founded in 1870 by a group of American citizens including businessmen, financiers, leading artists, and thinkers. The museum opened to the public on February 20, 1872, initially housed in a building on Fifth Avenue. In 1880, it moved to its current location in Central Park along Manhattan's Museum Mile. The iconic Beaux-Arts facade was completed in 1902, designed by architect Richard Morris Hunt.

## Significance
- One of the world's largest and most comprehensive art museums
- Houses over 2 million works spanning 5,000 years of world culture
- Annual attendance exceeds 7 million visitors, making it one of the most visited museums globally
- Serves as a cultural landmark and educational institution for New York City and the world
- Plays a crucial role in art preservation, scholarship, and public education

## Main Collections
The Met's collection is organized into 17 curatorial departments:

### Ancient Art
- Egyptian Art: One of the finest collections outside Cairo, including the Temple of Dendur
- Greek and Roman Art: Sculptures, vases, and artifacts from antiquity
- Ancient Near Eastern Art: Objects from Mesopotamia, Iran, and surrounding regions

### European Art
- European Paintings: Masterpieces by Rembrandt, Vermeer, Van Gogh, Monet, and many others
- European Sculpture and Decorative Arts: Furniture, tapestries, and decorative objects
- Arms and Armor: Medieval and Renaissance weaponry and armor

### American Art
- American Wing: Paintings, sculpture, and decorative arts from the colonial period through early 20th century
- Includes works by John Singer Sargent, Winslow Homer, and other American masters

### Asian Art
- Comprehensive collection spanning China, Japan, Korea, India, and Southeast Asia
- Includes paintings, sculptures, ceramics, and textiles

### Islamic Art
- Artworks from the Islamic world spanning 1,300 years
- Calligraphy, ceramics, metalwork, and textiles

### Modern and Contemporary Art
- Works from the early 20th century to the present
- Includes pieces by Picasso, Pollock, Rothko, and contemporary artists

### Special Features
- The Met Cloisters: A branch museum dedicated to medieval European art and architecture
- Costume Institute: Houses fashion and costume collections (hosts the annual Met Gala)
- Musical Instruments: Rare and historic instruments from around the world

## Architecture and Campus
The main building on Fifth Avenue covers 2 million square feet and includes:
- The Great Hall entrance
- Multiple wings and galleries
- The rooftop garden with seasonal installations and city views
- Extensive conservation and research facilities

The Met continues to expand its collections and programs, maintaining its mission to collect, preserve, study, and present significant works of art across all times and cultures for the enjoyment and education of the public.
