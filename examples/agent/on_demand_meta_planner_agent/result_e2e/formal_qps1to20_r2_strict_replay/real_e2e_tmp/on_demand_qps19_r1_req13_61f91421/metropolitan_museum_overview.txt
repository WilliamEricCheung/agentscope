# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: Overview

## History
The Metropolitan Museum of Art (commonly known as "The Met") was founded in 1870 by a group of American citizens including businessmen, financiers, and leading artists who sought to bring art and art education to the American people. The museum opened to the public on February 20, 1872, initially housed in a former military arsenal building. In 1880, it moved to its current location on the eastern edge of Central Park along Manhattan's Museum Mile. The original building was designed by Calvert Vaux and Jacob Wrey Mould, with significant later additions by architect Richard Morris Hunt and others.

## Significance
The Met is one of the world's largest and most comprehensive art museums, serving as a cultural landmark not just for New York City but for the entire United States. It is:
- The largest art museum in the United States
- Among the most visited museums globally
- A center for art education, conservation, and scholarly research
- A repository of human creativity spanning thousands of years and cultures worldwide

## Main Collections
The Met houses over 2 million works of art spanning 5,000 years of world culture from every part of the globe. Its major collections include:

- **European Paintings**: Masterpieces by Rembrandt, Vermeer, Van Gogh, Monet, and many other renowned artists
- **American Wing**: Decorative arts, paintings, and sculptures reflecting American history and culture
- **Egyptian Art**: One of the finest collections outside Cairo, including the Temple of Dendur
- **Asian Art**: Comprehensive holdings from China, Japan, Korea, India, and Southeast Asia
- **Islamic Art**: Exceptional collection spanning 13 centuries and three continents
- **Greek and Roman Art**: Classical antiquities including sculptures, vases, and jewelry
- **Arms and Armor**: Historical weapons and armor from around the world
- **Modern and Contemporary Art**: Works from the 20th and 21st centuries
- **Costume Institute**: Extensive collection of fashion and costume design

The museum also operates The Met Cloisters in Fort Tryon Park, dedicated exclusively to medieval European art and architecture, featuring elements from five medieval cloisters and gardens.

The Met continues to fulfill its mission of collecting, preserving, studying, exhibiting, and stimulating appreciation for works of art that collectively represent the broadest spectrum of human achievement at the highest level of quality.
