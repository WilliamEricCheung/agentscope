# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: Key Information Summary

## History
- Founded on April 13, 1870, when the New York State Legislature granted an Act of Incorporation
- First opened to the public on February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue
- Established by a group of Americans including philanthropists, artists, and businessmen with the goal of creating a national institution to inspire and educate the public
- Notable founders included John Taylor Johnston (first president), George Palmer Putnam (founding superintendent), Eastman Johnson, Frederic Edwin Church, Theodore Roosevelt Sr., and Henry Gurdon Marquand
- Moved to its current location in 1880
- Luigi Palma di Cesnola served as its first director from 1879 to 1904

## Location
- Main building located at 1000 Fifth Avenue, along Museum Mile on the eastern edge of Central Park on Manhattan's Upper East Side, New York City
- The main building is one of the world's largest art museums by area, measuring almost 1/4-mile long with more than 2 million square feet of floor space
- Second location: The Cloisters at Fort Tryon Park in Upper Manhattan, dedicated to medieval European art, architecture, and artifacts
- Third site: The Neue Galerie New York (set to become part of the Met in 2028), which will house early 20th century Austrian and German art

## Notable Collections
The Met houses approximately 1.5 million works divided into 17 curatorial departments:

### Geographically Designated Collections:
- **Ancient Near Eastern Art**: Over 7,000 pieces from Sumerian, Hittite, Assyrian, Babylonian, and Elamite cultures
- **Arts of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas**: More than 11,000 pieces housed in the Michael C. Rockefeller Wing
- **Asian Art**: Over 35,000 pieces spanning 4,000 years, including comprehensive collections of Chinese, Indian, and Cambodian art
- **Egyptian Art**: More than 26,000 pieces, including the famous Temple of Dendur and William the Faience Hippopotamus (the museum's informal mascot)
- **European Paintings**: Over 2,600 works from the 13th through early 20th centuries, including significant Impressionist and Post-Impressionist collections
- **Greek and Roman Art**: More than 17,000 objects, including the Monteleone chariot and Cycladic sculptures
- **Islamic Art**: One of the world's largest collections with 12,000 pieces from Spain to Central Asia

### Non-Geographically Designated Collections:
- **American Wing**: Comprehensive collection of American art from the 18th through early 20th century
- **Arms and Armor**: 14,000 objects spanning cultures from ancient Egypt to 20th century America
- **Costume Institute**: More than 35,000 costumes and accessories
- **Drawings and Prints**: Approximately 21,000 drawings and 1.2 million prints
- **Robert Lehman Collection**: 2,600 works considered "one of the most extraordinary private art collections ever assembled in the United States"
- **Medieval Art**: Over 10,000 objects split between the main building and The Cloisters
- **Modern and Contemporary Art**: 13,000 works including iconic pieces by Picasso, Pollock, and Johns
- **Musical Instruments**: 5,000 instruments from around the world, including Stradivari violins
- **Photographs**: More than 25,000 photographs from major collections including Stieglitz and Walker Evans

## Additional Facts
- By floor area, it is the fourth-largest museum in the world and the largest art museum in the Americas
- Most-visited museum in the United States with over 5.7 million visitors in fiscal year 2025
- The museum's first accessioned object was a Roman sarcophagus, still on display today
