# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: History, Significance, and Collections

## History
The Metropolitan Museum of Art (the Met) was founded in 1870 by a group of Americans including philanthropists, artists, and businessmen with the goal of creating a national institution that would inspire and educate the public. The New York State Legislature granted the museum an Act of Incorporation on April 13, 1870.

The museum first opened on February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue. Its initial collection consisted of a Roman stone sarcophagus and 174 mostly European paintings. As the collection quickly outgrew the available space, the museum moved to its current location in Central Park in 1880. The main building at 1000 Fifth Avenue was constructed in stages, with the first portion built in 1880.

Key founders included John Taylor Johnston (railroad executive and first president), George Palmer Putnam (founding superintendent), Eastman Johnson (artist), Frederic Edwin Church (landscape painter), and Henry Gurdon Marquand (who donated important Old Masters paintings). Theodore Roosevelt Sr., father of President Theodore Roosevelt, was also among the founders.

In 1938, the museum expanded with The Cloisters, a separate building in Fort Tryon Park dedicated solely to medieval art, which was a principal project of John D. Rockefeller Jr. More recently, the museum announced plans to merge with the nearby Neue Galerie New York in 2028, which will become the Met's newest location focused on early 20th century Austrian and German art.

## Significance
The Metropolitan Museum of Art holds tremendous cultural and historical significance:

- It is the fourth-largest museum in the world by floor area and the largest art museum in the Americas
- With approximately 5.7 million visitors in fiscal year 2025, it was the most-visited museum in the United States and the fifth-most visited art museum globally
- The museum houses approximately 1.5 million works of art spanning 5,000 years of world culture
- Its collection is divided into 17 curatorial departments with specialized staff
- The main building covers more than 2 million square feet (190,000 m²), making it more than 20 times the size of the original 1880 building
- The museum was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1986, recognizing both its monumental architecture and its importance as a cultural institution

## Main Collections
The Met's encyclopedic collection encompasses art from around the world and across time periods:

### Geographically Designated Collections
1. **Ancient Near Eastern Art**: Over 7,000 pieces representing cultures from the Neolithic Period through Late Antiquity, including Sumerian, Hittite, Assyrian, Babylonian, and Elamite works.

2. **Egyptian Art**: More than 26,000 objects from the Paleolithic era through the Ptolemaic era, displayed in 40 Egyptian galleries. The centerpiece is the Temple of Dendur, given to the United States by Egypt in 1965.

3. **Greek and Roman Art**: Over 17,000 objects including the museum's first accessioned object (a Roman sarcophagus). Highlights include the Amathus sarcophagus and the Monteleone chariot.

4. **Asian Art**: More than 35,000 pieces spanning 4,000 years, considered the most comprehensive collection in the US. Includes significant holdings of Chinese, Indian, Cambodian, Japanese, Korean, and Southeast Asian art.

5. **Islamic Art**: One of the world's largest collections of Islamic art with approximately 12,000 objects from Spain to Central Asia, including secular items, religious works, and miniature paintings.

6. **Arts of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas**: Over 11,000 pieces housed in the Michael C. Rockefeller Wing, including works from sub-Saharan Africa, the Pacific Islands, and the Americas dating from 3,000 BCE to the present.

### European Collections
1. **European Paintings**: Over 2,600 works from the 13th through early 20th centuries, including significant holdings of Old Masters, Impressionist, and Post-Impressionist works.

2. **European Sculpture and Decorative Arts**: Over 50,000 pieces from the 15th through early 20th centuries, including period rooms, furniture, jewelry, and tapestries.

### American Collections
1. **American Wing**: Comprehensive collection of American art from the 18th through early 20th century, including the addition of Indigenous American art since 2018.

2. **Modern and Contemporary Art**: Approximately 13,000 works by European and American artists, including iconic pieces like Picasso's portrait of Gertrude Stein and Jackson Pollock's Autumn Rhythm.

### Specialized Collections
1. **Arms and Armor**: One of the museum's most popular collections with 14,000 objects spanning cultures from ancient Egypt to 20th-century America.

2. **Costume Institute**: Over 35,000 costumes and accessories, known for hosting the annual Met Gala and thematic exhibitions.

3. **Musical Instruments**: Approximately 5,000 instruments from around the world, including Stradivari violins and the oldest surviving piano (1720).

4. **Drawings and Prints**: About 21,000 drawings, 1.2 million prints, and 12,000 illustrated books.

5. **Photographs**: Over 25,000 photographs from major collections including those of Alfred Stieglitz and Walker Evans.

6. **Robert Lehman Collection**: A "museum within the museum" containing 2,600 works donated by banker Robert Lehman, particularly strong in Italian Renaissance paintings.

7. **Medieval Art and The Cloisters**: Over 10,000 objects of Western art from the 4th through early 16th centuries, with The Cloisters housing 5,000 strictly medieval European works.
