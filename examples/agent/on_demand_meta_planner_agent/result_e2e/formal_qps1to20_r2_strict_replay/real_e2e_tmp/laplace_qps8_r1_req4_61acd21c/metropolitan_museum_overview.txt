# The Metropolitan Museum of Art - Comprehensive Overview

## History
- Founded in 1870 by a group of American citizens including businessmen, financiers, and leading artists
- Opened to the public on February 20, 1872, initially housed in a building on Fifth Avenue
- Moved to its current location on the eastern edge of Central Park along Manhattan's Museum Mile in 1880
- The main building was designed by Calvert Vaux and Jacob Wrey Mould, with numerous additions over the decades

## Significance
- One of the world's largest and most comprehensive art museums
- Often referred to as "The Met"
- Houses over 2 million works spanning 5,000 years of world culture
- Receives millions of visitors annually, making it one of the most visited museums globally
- Serves as a cultural cornerstone of New York City and the United States

## Main Collections

### European Paintings
Masterpieces by Rembrandt, Vermeer, Van Gogh, Monet, and other renowned artists

### American Wing
Comprehensive collection of American art from the colonial period through early 20th century

### Ancient Egyptian Art
One of the finest collections outside Cairo, including the Temple of Dendur

### Greek and Roman Art
Extensive collection of antiquities

### Asian Art
Significant holdings from China, Japan, Korea, India, and Southeast Asia

### Islamic Art
Important collection spanning from Spain to India

### Arms and Armor
Notable collection of medieval and Renaissance weaponry

### Costume Institute
Famous for its fashion exhibitions and annual Met Gala

### Modern and Contemporary Art
Works from the 20th and 21st centuries

## Additional Information
The Met also operates The Met Cloisters in Fort Tryon Park, dedicated to medieval European art and architecture.
