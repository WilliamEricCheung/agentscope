# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: Overview

## History and Founding
The Metropolitan Museum of Art (the Met) was founded in 1870 by a group of Americans including philanthropists, artists, and businessmen with the goal of creating a national institution that would inspire and educate the public. The museum first opened on February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue before moving to its current location in 1880. Key founders included John Taylor Johnston (first president), George Palmer Putnam (founding superintendent), and artists Eastman Johnson and Frederic Edwin Church. Theodore Roosevelt Sr., father of President Theodore Roosevelt, was also among the founders.

## Significance
The Met is one of the world's most important cultural institutions:
- By floor area, it is the fourth-largest museum in the world and the largest art museum in the Americas
- It was the most-visited museum in the United States and the fifth-most visited art museum globally, with over 5.7 million visitors in fiscal year 2025
- The museum's main building was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1986
- It houses approximately 1.5 million works of art spanning 5,000 years of world culture

## Main Collections
The Met's encyclopedic collection is organized into 17 curatorial departments:

1. **Ancient Near Eastern Art** - Over 7,000 pieces from Sumerian, Hittite, Assyrian, Babylonian, and other cultures
2. **Arts of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas** - More than 11,000 pieces housed in the Michael C. Rockefeller Wing
3. **Asian Art** - Over 35,000 pieces spanning 4,000 years, including comprehensive collections of Chinese, Indian, and Cambodian art
4. **Egyptian Art** - More than 26,000 objects including the famous Temple of Dendur
5. **European Paintings** - Over 2,600 works from the 13th through early 20th centuries, including masterpieces by Rembrandt, Vermeer, and Van Gogh
6. **American Wing** - Art from the 18th through early 20th century
7. **Islamic Art** - One of the world's largest collections with 12,000 objects
8. **Greek and Roman Art** - More than 17,000 objects dating back to the museum's founding
9. **Modern and Contemporary Art** - Including iconic works by Picasso, Pollock, and Johns
10. **Arms and Armor** - 14,000 objects spanning cultures and time periods
11. **Costume Institute** - Over 35,000 costumes and accessories
12. **Musical Instruments** - About 5,000 instruments from around the world
13. **Photographs** - More than 25,000 photographs
14. **Drawings and Prints** - 21,000 drawings and 1.2 million prints
15. **Medieval Art** - Split between the main building and The Cloisters
16. **European Sculpture and Decorative Arts** - Over 50,000 pieces
17. **Robert Lehman Collection** - 2,600 works donated in 1969

The museum also operates The Cloisters in Fort Tryon Park, dedicated to medieval European art, and plans to merge with the Neue Galerie New York in 2028, which will add significant Austrian and German art to its holdings.
