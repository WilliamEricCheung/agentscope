# The Metropolitan Museum of Art: Overview

## History
The Metropolitan Museum of Art (the Met) was founded in 1870 by a group of American philanthropists, artists, and businessmen with the goal of creating a national institution to inspire and educate the public. The museum first opened on February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue before moving to its current location on Fifth Avenue along Central Park's eastern edge in 1880. The founders included notable figures such as Theodore Roosevelt Sr. (father of President Theodore Roosevelt), railroad executive John Taylor Johnston, and artist Eastman Johnson.

## Significance
- The Met is the fourth-largest museum in the world by floor area and the largest art museum in the Americas
- It houses approximately 1.5 million works of art across 17 curatorial departments
- With over 5.7 million visitors annually (as of fiscal year 2025), it is the most-visited museum in the United States and the fifth-most visited art museum globally
- The main building spans approximately 2 million square feet and is considered one of the world's largest art museums
- The museum was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1986

## Main Collections

### Geographically Designated Collections
1. **Ancient Near Eastern Art**: Over 7,000 pieces spanning from the Neolithic Period through Late Antiquity, including works from Sumerian, Hittite, Assyrian, Babylonian, and Elamite cultures.

2. **Egyptian Art**: More than 26,000 objects from the Paleolithic era through the Ptolemaic era, featuring the iconic Temple of Dendur as its centerpiece.

3. **Greek and Roman Art**: Over 17,000 objects including the monumental Amathus sarcophagus and the Monteleone chariot.

4. **Asian Art**: One of the most comprehensive collections in the US with more than 35,000 pieces spanning 4,000 years, featuring significant holdings of Chinese, Indian, Cambodian, and Japanese art.

5. **Islamic Art**: Approximately 12,000 works from Islamic cultures ranging from Spain to Central Asia, including miniature paintings, ceramics, textiles, and calligraphy.

6. **Arts of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas**: Over 11,000 pieces housed in the Michael C. Rockefeller Wing, representing diverse cultural traditions from 40,000-year-old indigenous Australian rock paintings to ceremonial objects from the Nigerian Court of Benin.

### European Collections
1. **European Paintings**: Over 2,600 works from the 13th through early 20th centuries, including significant holdings of Old Masters, Impressionist, and Post-Impressionist works by artists like Rembrandt, Vermeer, Monet, Van Gogh, and Cézanne.

2. **European Sculpture and Decorative Arts**: Over 50,000 pieces from the 15th through early 20th centuries, featuring period rooms and notable works by Bernini and Rodin.

3. **Robert Lehman Collection**: One of the most extraordinary private art collections ever assembled in the US, with particular strengths in Italian Renaissance paintings.

### American Collections
1. **American Wing**: Comprehensive collection of American art from the 18th through early 20th century, including paintings, sculptures, and decorative arts.

2. **Art of Native America**: First displayed in the American Wing in 2018, featuring Indigenous American art and artifacts.

### Specialized Collections
1. **Modern and Contemporary Art**: Approximately 13,000 works by European and American artists, including iconic pieces by Picasso, Pollock, and Johns.

2. **Arms and Armor**: One of the museum's most popular collections with 14,000 objects spanning cultures and time periods, including armor belonging to European royalty.

3. **Costume Institute**: Over 35,000 costumes and accessories, known for hosting the annual Met Gala and thematic exhibitions.

4. **Musical Instruments**: About 5,000 instruments from around the world, including Stradivari violins and the oldest surviving piano (1720).

5. **Drawings and Prints**: Approximately 21,000 drawings, 1.2 million prints, and 12,000 illustrated books.

6. **Photographs**: Over 25,000 photographs from major collections including those of Alfred Stieglitz and Walker Evans.

### The Cloisters
A separate branch of the Met located in Fort Tryon Park, dedicated exclusively to medieval European art and architecture. The collection includes the famous "Hunt of the Unicorn" tapestries and the "Belles Heures" illuminated manuscript.
