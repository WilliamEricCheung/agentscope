# Metropolitan Museum of Art - Key Information Summary

## History
- Founded in 1870 by a group of Americans including philanthropists, artists, and businessmen
- Goal was to create a national institution that would inspire and educate the public
- First opened on February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue
- Moved to current location in 1880
- The museum's first accessioned object was a Roman sarcophagus
- Initial collection consisted of 174 mostly European paintings
- Luigi Palma di Cesnola served as first director (1879-1904)

## Location
- Main building: 1000 Fifth Avenue, along Museum Mile on the eastern edge of Central Park
- Manhattan's Upper East Side, New York City
- Second location: The Cloisters at Fort Tryon Park in Upper Manhattan (medieval European art)
- Third site: Neue Galerie New York (to become part of the Met in 2028)
- The main building measures almost 1/4-mile long with over 2 million square feet of floor space
- Land granted by City of New York in 1871 between East Park Drive, Fifth Avenue, and 79th-85th Street transverse roads

## Notable Features
- Fourth-largest museum in the world by floor area and largest art museum in the Americas
- Permanent collection of approximately 1.5 million works (down from over 2 million in 2000)
- Collection divided into 17 curatorial departments
- Most-visited museum in the United States with 5,727,258 visitors in fiscal year 2025
- Houses encyclopedic collections spanning from ancient Near East and Egypt through contemporary world
- Notable collections include:
  - European Old Masters paintings and sculptures
  - Extensive American, modern, and contemporary art
  - African, Asian, Oceanian, Byzantine, and Islamic art
  - Musical instruments, costumes, decorative arts and textiles
  - Antique weapons and armor from around the world
  - Period rooms ranging from 1st-century Rome through modern American design
- Famous highlights include:
  - Temple of Dendur (Egyptian collection centerpiece)
  - William the Faience Hippopotamus (informal mascot)
  - The Cloisters' seven tapestries depicting the Hunt of the Unicorn
  - Astor Court (complete Ming Dynasty-style garden court)
  - Robert Lehman Collection (one of the most extraordinary private collections ever assembled in the US)
