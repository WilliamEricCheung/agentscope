# Metropolitan Museum of Art - Wikipedia Summary

## History
The Metropolitan Museum of Art (the Met) was founded in 1870 by a group of American philanthropists, artists, and businessmen with the goal of creating a national institution to inspire and educate the public. It first opened on February 20, 1872, at 681 Fifth Avenue before moving to its current location in 1880. The museum's initial collection consisted of 174 mostly European paintings and a Roman sarcophagus (which remains on display as the museum's first accessioned object).

## Location
The main building is located at 1000 Fifth Avenue along Museum Mile on the eastern edge of Central Park in Manhattan's Upper East Side, New York City. The museum also operates The Cloisters at Fort Tryon Park in Upper Manhattan, which houses medieval European art, and plans to merge with the nearby Neue Galerie New York in 2028. The main building spans over 2 million square feet, making it one of the world's largest art museums.

## Notable Features
- Fourth-largest museum in the world by floor area and the largest art museum in the Americas
- Houses approximately 1.5 million works divided into 17 curatorial departments
- Most-visited museum in the United States with nearly 5.7 million visitors annually
- Encyclopedic collection spanning from ancient civilizations to contemporary art
- Notable highlights include the Temple of Dendur (Egyptian collection centerpiece), William the Faience Hippopotamus (informal mascot), the Astor Court (Ming Dynasty-style garden), and the Robert Lehman Collection
- Comprehensive holdings of European Old Masters, American art, African, Asian, Oceanian, Byzantine, and Islamic art
- Extensive collections of musical instruments, costumes, decorative arts, textiles, and antique weapons and armor
- Features period rooms ranging from 1st-century Rome through modern American design
